# R3.09 Change lifecycle states

Phase 3 · Size M, at its top end, with the scope lines below · Depends on: R2.03 (`OpMeta` and the `reef.*` attributes, `Manager::rewrite`, `UndoOperation`'s revert and supersession rules), and builds on R2.02 (revisions, `Change`, `trunk()`), R2.08 (remote tokens and `remote/rules.rs`), R2.09 (the operation stream), R2.12 (reads at an operation), R2.16 (GC's operation-id map), R2.18 (the web UI's state colors) and R3.03/R3.04 (identities, provenance sessions) · Unblocks: R3.10, R3.12, R3.13, R3.31, R4.07; through them R3.11, R3.33 and R3.34

## Context

The design names the lifecycle once, in the data model's Proposals paragraph: "A proposal is a change or stack put forward for main with its evidence. It is not a separate object; it is the change in its proposed state. A change moves through draft, proposed, queued, landed, and one of declined, withdrawn, or superseded, and the operation log records each transition. The change ID identifies the proposal, so there is no second number to track." The Human interface section adds that a reviewer "accepts the proposal or a prefix of its stack", that the triage inbox watches "proposals you asked for, in flight", and that every live view uses "one state color per object… Draft, proposed, queued, testing, landed, failed, conflicted". The Stacks paragraph says what happens to dependents when a dependency is "declined or withdrawn" or "superseded".

Seven later tickets read the state this ticket records:

- **R3.10** (stacks) proposes and lands a stack "as one unit with prefix landing".
- **R3.11** returns dependents to their owner when a dependency is declined or withdrawn, and rebases them onto the superseding change when it is superseded.
- **R3.12** attaches comments to proposals and resumes "the originating agent's session".
- **R3.13** (landing service, local) takes proposals into a queue and lands them, "refusing anything but `ALLOW`".
- **R3.31** (proposal view) offers "Accept, decline, comment".
- **R3.33** (triage inbox) lists proposals needing someone, in flight, and landed since the reader last looked.
- **R4.07** publishes proposals with their evidence to a store.

R2.03 left this ticket a seam: "R3.09 adds its lifecycle kinds as new `reef.kind` values and `OperationKind` values, written through `OpMeta`." R3.07 left two: "R3.09 adds `change.state`" to the policy input, and "its accept transition may sign `signoff` evidence for the accepting person".

So this ticket builds:

1. **The model**: seven states, the transitions between them, and who may perform each, written down so that R3.10 to R3.13 extend it rather than reshape it.
2. **The record**: every transition is a jj operation carrying the transition in a `reef.lifecycle` attribute, and a row in `reef.sqlite3` that keeps the state through GC, undo and restore.
3. **The client transitions**: propose, withdraw, decline and supersede through `ChangeService.TransitionChange`; and one the daemon performs itself, recording a change as landed when it shows up on `trunk()`.
4. **The reads**: the state on every `Change` the API returns, `ListProposals`, `ListTransitions`, and transitions on every `Operation`.
5. **The surface**: `reef propose` and `reef proposal`, the state in `reef show` and `reef log`, and the web UI's existing state colors fed from it.

Queueing and landing are defined here and performed by R3.13. Nothing in this ticket lands a change or lets a state authorize a landing.

### The states

| State | Means | Recorded by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DRAFT` | Work not put forward. Every change starts here and has no row. | nothing; the absence of a row |
| `PROPOSED` | Put forward for its destination with its evidence; waiting on review, evidence or the landing service. | a client (`propose`) |
| `QUEUED` | Taken by the landing service; it will try to land the recorded commit. | the landing service (R3.13) |
| `LANDED` | Its commit is on the destination. Final. | the landing service (R3.13), or the daemon on seeing it on `trunk()` (decision 5) |
| `DECLINED` | A reviewer said no. | a client (`decline`) |
| `WITHDRAWN` | Its proposer took it back. | a client (`withdraw`) |
| `SUPERSEDED` | Replaced by another change, which the transition names. Final. | a client (`supersede`) |

- **Unrecorded changes read as derived.** A change with no row is `DRAFT`, unless it is `immutable` (an ancestor of `trunk() | tags()`), which reads `LANDED`; both with `recorded: false`. That is the rule the web UI already applies (`changeState`), so the repository's existing history reads as landed without writing a row for each commit, and nothing is recorded that did not happen through Reef.
- **Testing, batched, bisecting and failed are not lifecycle states.** They are what the landing pipeline is doing to a queued change (R3.14, R3.34), reported as presence (R3.05's `JOB` kind) and in R3.34's view. A failed landing returns the change to `PROPOSED` with a cause (R3.13). The design's "one state color per object" list mixes the two; the web UI keeps doing so, from both sources.
- **"Accept" is not a transition.** R3.07 already makes a reviewer's acceptance `signoff` evidence on the change's tree (ADR 0061 decision 4), which `reef evidence add --kind signoff --actor human` signs today and which policy counts. R3.31's Accept button signs one, and R3.13 queues the change when policy allows. A separate `ACCEPTED` state would be a second, unsigned record of the same fact, and policy would have to choose between them.

### The transitions

| Verb | From | To | Who | Performed in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `propose` | `DRAFT`, `WITHDRAWN`, `DECLINED` | `PROPOSED` | any caller | R3.09 |
| `propose` (update) | `PROPOSED` | `PROPOSED` | the proposer, or a human | R3.09 |
| `withdraw` | `PROPOSED`, `QUEUED` | `WITHDRAWN` | the proposer, or a human | R3.09 |
| `decline` | `PROPOSED`, `QUEUED` | `DECLINED` | a human (decision 6) | R3.09 |
| `supersede` | `PROPOSED`, `QUEUED` | `SUPERSEDED` | the proposer, or a human; names the superseding change | R3.09 |
| observed landing | any recorded state but `LANDED` | `LANDED` | the daemon, on seeing the change on `trunk()` | R3.09 (decision 5) |
| `enqueue` | `PROPOSED` | `QUEUED` | the landing service, when policy allows | defined here; R3.13 |
| `dequeue` | `QUEUED` | `PROPOSED` | the landing service: amended, blocked, conflicted, failed checks | defined here; R3.13, R3.14 |
| `land` | `QUEUED` | `LANDED` | the landing service, in the operation that moves the destination | defined here; R3.13 |

- **A proposal is the change's, across rounds.** Re-proposing a withdrawn or declined change keeps its change id and increments `round` (1 for the first proposal). R3.12's comments and R3.31's history attach to the change id and carry the round they were made in. `LANDED` and `SUPERSEDED` are final: no transition leaves them, and a change in either is refused with `TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.
- **The only way back to `DRAFT` is undoing a first `propose`** (below). A withdrawn change that the agent keeps editing stays `WITHDRAWN` until it is proposed again: "not proposed now, and was".
- **Re-proposing an unchanged proposal writes nothing.** A transition to the state the change is in, with the same commit and destination, succeeds with no operation, as `DescribeChange` does for unchanged text; retries are safe. `propose` while `PROPOSED` with a different commit (the change was amended or rebased) records a `PROPOSED → PROPOSED` transition that updates the recorded commit: the agent's way of saying "ready again" after review (R3.12).
- **The commit.** Every transition records the change's visible commit at that moment. A proposal follows its change: amending a proposed change keeps it `PROPOSED`, and reads report `amended: true` while the visible commit differs from the recorded one, until the next `propose`. `QUEUED` pins the commit: R3.13 lands that commit or dequeues the change, never lands a later one silently. Evidence and sign-offs bind to trees (R3.01, R3.07), so an amendment never inherits the old tree's evidence; R3.15 owns carrying any of it across.
- **What a proposal may be.** `propose` refuses, as `FAILED_PRECONDITION`: an immutable commit (`IMMUTABLE_REVISION`; it has landed), a hidden one (`HIDDEN_REVISION`), the root, an empty change (`CHANGE_EMPTY`, hint "did you mean `@-`?", since a workspace's `@` is often a fresh empty change), a conflicted tree (`CHANGE_CONFLICTED`; landing refuses conflicts, so the proposal could only fail), and one commit of a divergent change (`CHANGE_DIVERGENT`; resolve it with `reef abandon` first). A change whose ancestors are unlanded drafts may be proposed; whether its ancestors must be proposed with it is R3.10's.
- **The destination** is a bookmark name recorded with each `propose`: `trunk()`'s bookmark at that moment (`main`, or `main@origin` when there is no local one), or another existing local bookmark named in the request. It is recorded, not acted on: R3.13 lands onto it, and R4.07 publishes it. A repository with no `trunk()` and no named destination refuses with `NO_DESTINATION`.
- **`supersede`** names the superseding change by revision; it must resolve to one visible commit of another change, which need not be proposed. The transition records its change id as `superseded_by`. R3.11 rebases dependents onto it.
- **Queued changes can be withdrawn and declined.** R3.13 must take the repository's lifecycle lock (below) around its landing operation and land only a change still `QUEUED` at the recorded commit, so a withdrawal can never race a landing into both.

### Who may perform a transition

Every transition records who performed it: the `reef-agent` metadata (`agent`), the actor (`HUMAN`, `AGENT`, or `DAEMON` for the daemon's own), the operator identity, the provenance session when the request names a workspace (R3.04; R3.11 and R3.12 use it to find "the originating agent's session"), and the remote token when the call came through the remote listener.

- **On the socket**, one uid is one trust domain (ADR 0027): any caller may perform any client transition. The actor is declared in the request, and defaults to `AGENT` when the call carries an agent name and `HUMAN` otherwise, which is how `reef` run by a person and `reef` run under `REEF_AGENT` already differ. The identity is the named workspace's session identity, else the daemon's default person identity. All of it is recorded as declared, and `docs/proposals.md` says so (S-107).
- **Through the remote listener**, `TransitionChange` gets a new rule, `Rule::Lifecycle`, decided in the handler per verb, since the proto's one method carries all four: `propose` of a change in `DRAFT` is open to any `write` token; re-proposing, updating, withdrawing and superseding need the token that recorded the change's latest `propose` (`REMOTE_PROPOSAL_NOT_OWNED`); `decline` is refused (`REMOTE_HUMAN_REQUIRED`), because R3.03 never certifies `human` remotely and R3.43 is what makes a remote human verifiable. A remote actor is always `AGENT`. Undoing a transition through the remote listener needs the token that recorded it.
- **Daemon transitions** (observed landing here; enqueue, dequeue and land in R3.13) are not reachable through `TransitionChange`: `to` of `QUEUED`, `LANDED` or `DRAFT` is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, reason `TRANSITION_RESERVED`. A state never authorizes a landing: R3.13 evaluates policy whatever the state says (S-109).

### The record: an operation, and a row

Each transition is **recorded twice, in a fixed order**: a row in `reef.sqlite3`, then a jj operation that carries it, then the row is marked committed with the operation's id. The operation is what the design means by "the operation log records each transition": it is in `ListOperations`, the subscription stream, `reef op log`, the activity feed and replay. The row is what keeps the state when the operation is gone: GC drops operations older than its window, and reading the state of every proposal from the log would walk the whole log.

- **The operation.** An empty transaction (no view change) committed through `OpMeta` with kind `transition` (a new `OpKind::Transition`, `OPERATION_KIND_TRANSITION = 12`), the request's workspace and agent, the description `reef: <verb> change <change id hex>` (`reef: propose change …`, `withdraw`, `decline`, `supersede`, `land`), and a new attribute **`reef.lifecycle`** holding the transitions as compact JSON, format 1:

  ```json
  {"v":1,"transitions":[{"id":"trn_8f3a0c2d91e4b675","change":"<hex>","commit":"<hex>",
    "from":"proposed","to":"withdrawn","cause":"request","round":1,
    "reason":"…","superseded_by":"","destination":"main","undoes":""}]}
  ```

  A list, so that R3.10 records a stack's transitions in one operation and R3.13 records `land` in the operation that moves the destination; `OpMeta` gains `transitions: Vec<Transition>` for that. This ticket writes one per operation. `classify` learns the description prefixes, so a stripped attribute still classifies as `TRANSITION`. Who performed it beyond `agent` and the remote token (actor, identity, session) is in the row, not the attribute, which keeps operations small.
- **The rows.** Migration `0012_lifecycle.sql` (schema 12, renumbered at rebase if another ticket takes 12):

  ```sql
  CREATE TABLE lifecycle_transitions(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, id TEXT NOT NULL,    -- trn_<16 hex>
               seq INTEGER NOT NULL,                       -- per repository, in commit order
               status TEXT NOT NULL,                       -- pending | committed
               change_id TEXT NOT NULL, commit_id TEXT NOT NULL,
               from_state TEXT NOT NULL, to_state TEXT NOT NULL,
               cause TEXT NOT NULL,                        -- request | undo | observed (R3.13 adds landing)
               round INTEGER NOT NULL, reason TEXT, superseded_by TEXT, destination TEXT,
               undoes_operation_id TEXT, undoes_transition_id TEXT,
               operation_id TEXT, operation_collected INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
               agent TEXT, actor TEXT NOT NULL, identity_id TEXT, identity_name TEXT,
               session_id TEXT, remote_token TEXT, remote_token_name TEXT,
               created_at INTEGER NOT NULL, recorded_at INTEGER,   -- recorded_at: the operation's end
               PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, id)) WITHOUT ROWID;
  CREATE TABLE lifecycle(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, change_id TEXT NOT NULL,
               state TEXT NOT NULL, round INTEGER NOT NULL, commit_id TEXT NOT NULL,
               destination TEXT, superseded_by TEXT, transition_id TEXT NOT NULL,
               proposed_by_token TEXT, updated_at INTEGER NOT NULL,
               PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, change_id)) WITHOUT ROWID;
  -- indexes: lifecycle by (repo_id, state, updated_at DESC); transitions by (repo_id, change_id, seq),
  -- (repo_id, seq DESC), (repo_id, recorded_at), and (status) WHERE status = 'pending'
  ```

  `lifecycle` is the current state, one row per change that ever left `DRAFT`; it is updated in the same SQLite transaction that commits a transition row, and could be rebuilt from `lifecycle_transitions` alone. `reef repo forget` deletes both by repository; backups copy them whole (ADR 0020).
- **The write path** (`reef-daemon/src/lifecycle/`), for one change:
  1. Validate the request; read the agent (`caller_of`).
  2. When the revision uses `@` and `skip_snapshot` is not set, snapshot the workspace first, as reads do, so a proposal of `@` includes what is on disk.
  3. Take the repository's create lock shared (GC waits, as for every write) and then its **lifecycle lock**, a per-repository `tokio::sync::Mutex` that every transition, and R3.13's landing, holds from the state check to the commit.
  4. On the repository thread: refresh, resolve the revision and `superseded_by`, check `expected_commit_id` (R2.03's reasons) and the refusals above.
  5. Read the state; check `expected_state` (`EXPECTED_STATE_MISMATCH`), the table (`TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`), and the remote rule; detect a no-op.
  6. Insert the transition row as `pending`.
  7. Commit the empty operation on the repository thread and publish it.
  8. In one SQLite transaction, mark the row `committed` with `operation_id`, `recorded_at` and the next `seq`, and upsert `lifecycle`.
  9. Respond with the lifecycle, the transition, and the operation from `written_operation`.

  A failure at step 7 deletes the pending row and returns the error. Rows are never `pending` except across a crash between steps 6 and 8.
- **Start-up resolution.** A new start step, after repositories open and before the socket is bound: for each `pending` row, walk the repository's operations from its heads back to the row's `created_at` minus five minutes, looking for a `reef.lifecycle` transition with the row's id. Found: commit the row with that operation, as step 8. Not found: delete it. The walk is bounded by time and runs only when a pending row exists. A restored backup is consistent by the same rule: the backup copies SQLite before the operation files and the op heads last (design, Durability), so a committed row's operation is always in the copy, and a pending row resolves either way.
- **Reads ignore pending rows.**

### Reading the state

- **`Change.lifecycle`** (field 18, `ChangeLifecycle`) is filled on every `Change` the daemon returns: `GetChange`, `ListChanges`, `GetStatus`, and the write responses. One `IN` query per response joins the rows to the page's change ids, on the async side after the jj read. At an operation (`at_operation`, R2.12), the state is the latest committed transition with `recorded_at` at or before that operation's end, so replay and the smartlog's scrubber show proposals as they were. `amended` compares the recorded commit with the `Change`'s commit.
- **`ChangeLifecycle`**: `state`, `recorded`, `round`, `commit_id` (the recorded commit), `amended`, `destination`, `superseded_by`, and `last`, the latest `ChangeTransition`.
- **`ListProposals`** (READ): the `lifecycle` rows of a repository, filtered by states (default `PROPOSED` and `QUEUED`), change id prefixes and proposing agents, newest update first, paged. Each entry is the lifecycle and the change at head (absent, with `visible: false`, when the change has no visible commit any more, such as one the jj CLI abandoned). This is R3.33's "watching" and R3.31's list.
- **`ListTransitions`** (READ): committed transitions, filtered by change id prefixes, target states, causes and `recorded_after`, newest first, paged. It is the audit trail that outlives GC, and R3.33's "landed since you last looked".
- **`Operation.transitions`** (field 18, `repeated ChangeTransition`) is parsed from `reef.lifecycle` on every operation the daemon returns or streams, with no extra read. `OperationFilter.changes` matches a transition's change as it matches an effect's, and `kinds` takes `TRANSITION`, so `reef op watch --kind transition --change <id>` and MCP's `wait_for_operations` let an agent wait for its proposal to be landed or declined without polling.
- **Presence carries nothing new.** A state is durable; presence is not (ADR 0037). The landing pipeline's progress on a queued change is R3.05's `JOB` presence and R3.34's.

### Observed landing (decision 5)

Before R3.13, changes reach `main` the way they do today: `jj git push`, a fast-forward on the git host and a fetch, `jj bookmark set`. Without this, a proposal landed that way would stay `PROPOSED` for good, and R3.33's inbox would fill with landed work.

- A follower woken by `OpEvents::changes()` keeps, per repository, the commit `trunk()` resolved to when it last looked. When it moves (or at start), and the repository has recorded changes not `LANDED`, it evaluates `(change_id(c1) | … | change_id(cn)) & ::trunk()` once on the repository thread, in chunks of 500 ids.
- Each change found records `→ LANDED` through the write path with cause `observed`, actor `DAEMON`, no agent, the commit found on `trunk()`, and the destination `trunk()`'s bookmark. One operation per change, in `seq` order, so each is undoable or auditable on its own.
- It applies to any recorded state but `LANDED`: a withdrawn change that someone landed by hand has landed. Unrecorded changes are left alone; they read `LANDED` derived.
- A git host's squash or rebase merge gives the commits new change ids, so their proposals stay `PROPOSED`; `docs/proposals.md` says to withdraw or supersede them, and R3.17 (degraded landing to a git remote) is where Reef learns to follow such landings.

### Undo, GC and restore

- **Undo of a client transition.** `UndoOperation` of an operation whose `reef.lifecycle` holds transitions with cause `request` (and no view change) reverts them instead of the view: when each transition is still its change's latest committed one, it records the inverse (`to → from`, the old commit, destination and round restored) with cause `undo`, `undoes` naming the operation, in an operation of kind `UNDO` described `reef: undo operation <id>` carrying `reef.lifecycle`. Otherwise `OPERATION_SUPERSEDED`, R2.03's reason and hint: undo later transitions first. Undoing a first `propose` returns the change to `DRAFT` and deletes its `lifecycle` row (the transitions stay). Undoing the undo re-applies, as R2.03's undo does. The request's workspace is snapshotted first and reported as `snapshot`, as for every undo.
- **Never undoable here** (`OPERATION_NOT_UNDOABLE`): an observed landing ("it records what `trunk()` shows; move the bookmark back instead"), and an operation that both changed the view and carries transitions, which only R3.13 writes and R3.13 decides for.
- **Undo of other operations** does not touch rows. The one write that could strand a proposal, abandoning it, is refused (below); an abandon by the jj CLI leaves the row and reads report `visible: false`.
- **`AbandonChange`** of a change in `PROPOSED` or `QUEUED` that is not divergent is `FAILED_PRECONDITION`, reason `CHANGE_IN_REVIEW`, metadata `change_id` and `state`, with `next` `reef proposal withdraw <change>`. Abandoning one commit of a divergent change still resolves it (ADR 0031) and is allowed.
- **GC.** A new step right after the jj step, under the same pause: every `operation_id` in `lifecycle_transitions` is mapped through `GcOutcome.rewritten`; a row whose operation is not in the map (it was older than `keep_newer`) keeps its old id and gets `operation_collected = 1`, and reads return the id with `operation_collected: true`. Rows are never deleted by GC: they are a few hundred bytes per transition and they are the audit trail.
- **Restore.** SQLite and the operation log come from the same backup and agree (above); `meta.rematerialize_pending` changes nothing here.

### The API: API revision 21

In `changes.proto`, `ChangeService` gains:

```proto
// A change's lifecycle (R3.09, ADR 0063). DRAFT has no record; LANDED and SUPERSEDED are final.
enum LifecycleState { LIFECYCLE_STATE_UNSPECIFIED = 0; LIFECYCLE_STATE_DRAFT = 1; LIFECYCLE_STATE_PROPOSED = 2;
  LIFECYCLE_STATE_QUEUED = 3; LIFECYCLE_STATE_LANDED = 4; LIFECYCLE_STATE_DECLINED = 5;
  LIFECYCLE_STATE_WITHDRAWN = 6; LIFECYCLE_STATE_SUPERSEDED = 7; }
enum TransitionCause { TRANSITION_CAUSE_UNSPECIFIED = 0; TRANSITION_CAUSE_REQUEST = 1;
  TRANSITION_CAUSE_UNDO = 2; TRANSITION_CAUSE_OBSERVED = 3; }      // R3.13 adds LANDING
enum TransitionActor { TRANSITION_ACTOR_UNSPECIFIED = 0; TRANSITION_ACTOR_HUMAN = 1;
  TRANSITION_ACTOR_AGENT = 2; TRANSITION_ACTOR_DAEMON = 3; }
message ChangeTransition { string id = 1; string change_id = 2; string commit_id = 3;
  LifecycleState from = 4; LifecycleState to = 5; TransitionCause cause = 6; uint32 round = 7;
  string reason = 8; string superseded_by = 9; string destination = 10; string undoes_operation_id = 11;
  string operation_id = 12; bool operation_collected = 13; google.protobuf.Timestamp recorded_at = 14;
  string agent = 15; TransitionActor actor = 16; string identity = 17; string identity_id = 18;
  string session_id = 19; RemoteCaller remote = 20; }
message ChangeLifecycle { LifecycleState state = 1; bool recorded = 2; uint32 round = 3; string commit_id = 4;
  bool amended = 5; string destination = 6; string superseded_by = 7; ChangeTransition last = 8; bool visible = 9; }
// Change gains: ChangeLifecycle lifecycle = 18;

rpc TransitionChange(TransitionChangeRequest) returns (TransitionChangeResponse) { ACCESS_WRITE }
rpc ListProposals(ListProposalsRequest) returns (ListProposalsResponse) { ACCESS_READ; NO_SIDE_EFFECTS }
rpc ListTransitions(ListTransitionsRequest) returns (ListTransitionsResponse) { ACCESS_READ; NO_SIDE_EFFECTS }

message TransitionChangeRequest { string repo = 1; string workspace = 2;
  string revision = 3;                 // required; `@` needs `workspace`
  LifecycleState to = 4;               // PROPOSED, WITHDRAWN, DECLINED or SUPERSEDED
  LifecycleState expected_state = 5;   // unspecified skips the check
  string expected_commit_id = 6;       // as DescribeChange's
  string reason = 7;                   // at most 4 KiB of UTF-8, no control characters but newline and tab
  string superseded_by = 8;            // a revision; required for SUPERSEDED, refused otherwise
  string destination = 9;              // PROPOSED only; a local bookmark; empty for trunk()'s
  TransitionActor actor = 10;          // HUMAN or AGENT; unspecified: AGENT with an agent name, else HUMAN
  bool skip_snapshot = 11; }
message TransitionChangeResponse { ChangeLifecycle lifecycle = 1; ChangeTransition transition = 2;
  Operation operation = 3;             // unset when nothing changed
  Operation snapshot = 4; Change change = 5; }
message ListProposalsRequest { string repo = 1; repeated LifecycleState states = 2; repeated string changes = 3;
  repeated string agents = 4; int32 page_size = 5; string page_token = 6; }
message ProposalEntry { ChangeLifecycle lifecycle = 1; string change_id = 2; Change change = 3; }
message ListProposalsResponse { repeated ProposalEntry entries = 1; string next_page_token = 2; }
message ListTransitionsRequest { string repo = 1; repeated string changes = 2; repeated LifecycleState to = 3;
  repeated TransitionCause causes = 4; google.protobuf.Timestamp recorded_after = 5; int32 page_size = 6;
  string page_token = 7; }
message ListTransitionsResponse { repeated ChangeTransition transitions = 1; string next_page_token = 2; }
```

## Risks

- **Two records can disagree.** Mitigated by the write order, the pending row, start-up resolution, and test 9; the rows can be rebuilt from `lifecycle_transitions` if `lifecycle` is ever wrong.
- **Observed landing writes operations the user did not ask for.** One per landed proposal, only for recorded changes; they are attributed to the daemon and say so.
- **Declared actors on the socket** mean a decline's actor is only as true as the caller (S-107), as for evidence's actor today; R3.43 fixes it for humans.
- **The state is per daemon until R4.07.** Two machines proposing the same change keep two records; the store reconciles them.
- **Empty operations add to the log.** One per transition, a few hundred bytes; GC treats them like any other.

## Choices this brief made, and why

- **A row and an operation**, because the operation is the design's record and the stream every live view reads, while the row is what survives GC and answers "every open proposal" in one query.
- **Draft unrecorded, landed derived**, because every change starts as a draft and most of trunk landed before Reef; recording them would invent history.
- **Proposals follow their change, queued pins it**, because a review comment's fix is an amendment and should not unpropose the change, while what the landing service lands must be exactly what it checked.
- **Accept as sign-off evidence**, because R3.07 already counts signed sign-offs and a state would be an unsigned copy.
- **Record everything, enforce what can be enforced**, because on one uid nothing can be enforced and the remote listener is where tokens tell callers apart.
- **Observed landing**, because before R3.13 every landing happens outside Reef, and a proposal that never leaves `PROPOSED` makes the inbox wrong.
- **Clients never record `QUEUED` or `LANDED`**, so that a state can never stand in for the landing gate.

## Departures in the implementation

Found while building it (2026-11-08):

1. **The lifecycle types live in a new `lifecycle.proto`** (`LifecycleState`, `TransitionCause`, `TransitionActor`, `ChangeTransition`, `ChangeLifecycle`), imported by `changes.proto` and `operations.proto`: `Operation.transitions` needs `ChangeTransition`, and `changes.proto` already imports `operations.proto`. 2. **`GetStatus` at an operation leaves `lifecycle` unset**; at head it is filled. `GetChange` and `ListChanges` fill it at an operation as planned.
3. **An observed landing is noticed when the daemon next loads the repository**: after a request, a snapshot, or a subscription's poll of the op heads (ADR 0036). Another `jj` writer's bookmark move alone wakes nothing until then. The follower reads the database first and resolves `trunk()` only for repositories with recorded changes not landed.
4. **`AbandonChange`'s refusal runs before the write, without the lifecycle lock.** Taking it around `Manager::rewrite`, which takes the create lock inside, would order the two locks the other way from a transition's and deadlock behind GC's queued writer. A proposal racing an abandon can therefore leave a recorded change with no visible commit, which reads `visible: false`.
5. **A proposal keeps its destination** when proposed again without `--to`, instead of taking `trunk()`'s again.
6. **The remote rule is checked after the transition table**, so a refused transition says why before it says whose it is; a no-op through another token is refused too.
7. **`reef proposal list --by AGENT`**, not `--agent`, which is the global flag naming the caller.
8. **An undo of a transition records its agent, actor and token**, not an identity or session.
9. **Test 14 (backup and restore) is not a test of its own.** The write order and test 9 cover what a restore can see: a committed row's operation is always older than the SQLite copy, and a pending row resolves at the restored daemon's first start.
10. **From the code review** (the PR's table lists every finding):
    - Transitions and undos run in a task of their own, so a dropped call cannot leave an operation without its row; a pending row committed late at start changes the state only when its `from` is still the change's state; a failed repository-thread call keeps the pending row for start-up to resolve.
    - An undo restores the proposal's owner with its state, and refuses to restore `QUEUED`; a proposal's owner is whoever opened its round, so updating the commit keeps it.
    - A recorded change with no visible commit can be withdrawn, declined or superseded by its change id (`TransitionChangeResponse.change` is then unset); proposing still needs a visible commit.
    - Observed landings look only at proposed and queued changes, so their cost follows the open proposals; a withdrawn or declined change landed by hand keeps its state, unlike the brief's "any recorded state but landed".
    - Rounds never repeat: a new round is one more than any before.
    - A read at an operation counts a transition whose operation ended in the same millisecond as after it, unless it is that operation.
    - `OperationFilter.kinds` with `TRANSITION` also keeps undos that carry transitions.
    - An empty or unknown `to` is `TRANSITION_TARGET_REQUIRED`, a new reason.
    - Undo routing sends an operation to the rows only when it changed nothing in the view.
    - The web UI names each transition (`proposed a change`, `withdrew a proposal`, …) and shows an abandoned proposal as abandoned.
    - A kept destination whose bookmark is gone falls back to `trunk()`'s.
11. **`buf` runs from the web UI's toolchain** (`web/node_modules/.bin/buf`, after `npm ci`): `buf lint`, `buf format --diff --exit-code` and `buf breaking` against `main` pass.

## Decisions for the user

Asked on 2026-11-08; the user accepted every recommendation below, and the brief's body follows them.

**Group 1: what a state is and where it lives**

1. **Storage.** Each transition is a jj operation (kind `transition`, attribute `reef.lifecycle`, no view change) and a row in `reef.sqlite3` (migration 12), written row-pending, operation, row-committed, with start-up resolution. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: the operation log alone, with state folded from it (GC drops old operations, and every read walks the log); a trailer in the commit (changes commit ids, leaks into git).
2. **States and graph.** Seven states; `DRAFT` has no row, and unrecorded immutable changes read `LANDED` (derived); withdrawn and declined changes can be proposed again under the same change id with `round` + 1; `LANDED` and `SUPERSEDED` are final. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: `DECLINED` final too (a reviewer's no is permanent; the agent makes a new change); all four ending states final.
3. **Amendments.** A proposal follows its change: amending keeps it `PROPOSED` with `amended: true` until the next `propose` updates the recorded commit; `QUEUED` pins the commit for R3.13. *Recommended*: as stated, so a fix for a review comment does not unpropose the change. Alternatives: any rewrite returns the change to `DRAFT`; or the proposal pins its commit and later edits are ignored until re-proposed.

**Group 2: who does what, and in which ticket**

4. **This ticket's transitions.** Clients propose, withdraw, decline and supersede through `TransitionChange`; enqueue, dequeue and land are defined here and only R3.13 records them (`TRANSITION_RESERVED` for clients); accept is `signoff` evidence, not a state. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: let clients set `QUEUED` and `LANDED` until R3.13 exists (a state would then mean nothing); or an `ACCEPTED` state recorded by a reviewer.
5. **Observed landing.** The daemon records `→ LANDED` (cause `observed`) when a recorded change reaches `trunk()` by any means (push, fetch, bookmark move). *Recommended*: yes; until R3.13, every landing happens outside Reef. Alternatives: record nothing until R3.13 (proposals landed by git stay `PROPOSED`); or derive `LANDED` at read time without an operation.
6. **Permissions.** On the socket anyone may do any client transition, recorded with a declared actor (default `AGENT` when an agent name is sent, else `HUMAN`), agent, identity and session. Through the remote listener: any `write` token proposes; only the proposing token re-proposes, withdraws, supersedes or undoes; `decline` is refused until R3.43 makes a remote human verifiable. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: let any `write` token decline remotely; or gate the socket by agent name too (advisory, since names are not authenticated).
7. **Abandoning a change in review.** `AbandonChange` refuses a proposed or queued change (`CHANGE_IN_REVIEW`, `next`: withdraw it), except to resolve divergence. *Recommended*: refuse. Alternatives: abandon withdraws it in the same operation; or allow it and report the proposal `visible: false`.

**Group 3: undo, GC, replay**

8. **Undo and history.** `UndoOperation` reverts a client transition when it is still its change's latest (else `OPERATION_SUPERSEDED`); observed landings are not undoable; GC maps the rows' operation ids and never deletes rows; `at_operation` reads show the state as of that operation. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: transitions are not undoable at all (use the inverse verb), which breaks "every mutation is undoable".

**Group 4: the surface**

9. **API.** `ChangeService` gains `TransitionChange` (WRITE, one method with a target state), `ListProposals` and `ListTransitions` (READ); `Change.lifecycle`, `Operation.transitions`, `OPERATION_KIND_TRANSITION`; API revision 21. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: a separate `LifecycleService`; one RPC per verb.
10. **CLI and MCP.** `reef propose` and `reef proposal list|show|withdraw|decline|supersede`, in `--help-all` only; state in `show` and `log`; MCP gets only the `transition` kind, and the RPCs go in `NOT_TOOLS` until R3.13. *Recommended*: as stated (tools/list is at its budget, and R3.44 is changing `reef mcp` now). Alternative: a `transition_change` MCP tool now, raising the budget by about 700 bytes.
11. **Web UI.** Feed `Change.lifecycle` into the existing state colors and add the feed's sentences, about 80 lines. *Recommended*: yes, since the smartlog and feed already have the colors. Alternative: nothing until R3.31.
12. **Policy input.** Leave `change.state` out of R3.07's input until R3.13. *Recommended*: defer, since at landing the state is always `QUEUED` and R3.08 is changing `reef-policy` now. Alternative: add it now.
