# R3.13 Landing service, local

Phase 3 · Size L, with the split in decision 1 · Depends on: R3.07 (`reef_policy::Policy::evaluate`, the policy read from a tree, the evaluation's source and `evidence_ids`), R3.09 (lifecycle rows, `record`, the lifecycle lock, `QUEUED` and `LANDED` reserved for the daemon), and builds on R2.03 (`Manager::rewrite`, `OpMeta`), R2.16 (GC's operation-id map), R3.01 and R3.02 (evidence by tree, retention's `PINNED`), R3.03 (key status at witness time), R3.04 (provenance and its retention) and R3.08 (the simulation's landing window, #246) · Unblocks: R3.11, R3.14, R3.16; through them R3.15, R3.17, R3.18, R3.20, R3.21, R3.34 and R4.11

## Context

The design's Landing section is the whole job: "The landing service verifies that each evidence object's snapshot hash matches the submitted tree and that the attestor is trusted by repository policy. It applies policy rules such as required test kinds, maximum diff size, forbidden paths, and human sign-off for designated areas. It refuses a change that still contains a conflict. Landing then rebases onto the current head. A rebase produces a new tree hash, so evidence computed on the submitted tree proves nothing about the rebased one … The landing service is therefore a merge queue. It batches rebased changes, asks the trusted executor to run the required checks on the batch head … If the rebase does not commute, the proposal returns to its owner with a conflict object." The batching, the executor run on the batch head and bisection are R3.14's; this ticket is the queue they run in, with a batch of one.

Two of Phase 3's exit criteria are this ticket's: "A proposal with valid evidence from a trusted attestor lands without human intervention under a permissive policy", and "A proposal with forged or untrusted evidence is refused with a structured reason."

Every earlier Phase 3 ticket left something here:

- **R3.01**: "R3.13 verifies that each evidence object's `tree_id` is the submitted tree, and refuses an `INVALID` signature with a structured reason."
- **R3.02**: "R3.13 pins the evidence a landing used, for good: a table or a lifecycle operation that `LiveSet`'s pin test reads (`retention.rs` has one function, `pinned`, for it to extend)."
- **R3.03**: "R3.13 refuses evidence whose identity status is not acceptable to policy, with a structured reason that names the status."
- **R3.04**: "R3.13 and R3.16 pin the changes a landing consumed, so their provenance never expires."
- **R3.05**: "R3.13 calls `executor::submit` in process with the kinds policy requires that the proposal's tree lacks … and pins the evidence it lands on. It links landed evidence to the change."
- **R3.06**: "R3.13 submits by revision, so every job has a commit … and treats `FAILED` as infrastructure."
- **R3.07**: "R3.13 calls `reef_policy::Policy::evaluate` inside its landing, with the policy from the branch it lands onto, refuses anything but `ALLOW`, pins `source.blob_id` and the `evidence_ids` of every satisfied rule, and reports the evaluation as its structured refusal."
- **R3.08** (#246): "R3.13 records landings with their pinned evidence and policy blob; simulation then gains a landing-record window."
- **R3.09**: "R3.13 adds cause `LANDING`, records `enqueue`, `dequeue` (with a reason: amended, blocked, conflicted, failed) and `land` (in the operation that moves the destination), holds the lifecycle lock around its landing, lands only the recorded commit, decides whether a landing is undoable, adds `change.state` to the policy input, and decides the root help, guide line and MCP tool for proposing."

So this ticket builds:

1. **The gate**: `LandingService.LandChange` and `reef land`, which judge a proposed change against the destination's policy and refuse anything but `ALLOW`, with a structured reason that names every rejected evidence object and why.
2. **The queue**: a per-repository queue of `QUEUED` changes and one worker that lands them in order, a batch of one at a time, behind a seam R3.14 widens.
3. **The landing**: rebase onto the destination's head when it moved, refuse a rebase that conflicts, judge again with the policy at that head, and move the destination in one operation that carries R3.09's `land` transition.
4. **The record**: a landing row per attempt with the evaluation, the policy bytes, the evidence pinned and the operations, so retention keeps what a landing used and R3.08 and R3.37 can read it.
5. **The surface**: `GetLanding`, `ListLandings`, `reef landing list|show`, the web UI's sentences, and the documents.

### What a landing is

A **landing** is one attempt to put queued changes onto a **destination**, a local bookmark, in one jj operation. This ticket lands one change per landing; the record and the code take a list, which R3.10's stacks and R3.14's batches fill.

| Outcome | Means | Lifecycle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BLOCKED` | The gate refused it: the change was never queued. | stays `PROPOSED`; no transition |
| `QUEUED` | Queued and not finished yet (a read while it is in flight). | `PROPOSED → QUEUED` (enqueue) |
| `LANDED` | The destination moved to the landed commit. | `QUEUED → LANDED` (land), in the operation that moved it |
| `DEQUEUED` | Queued, then returned, with a reason. | `QUEUED → PROPOSED` (dequeue) |
| `CANCELLED` | A client withdrew, declined or superseded the change while it was queued. | the client's transition; none from the landing |

A reason (`LandingReason`) says why a landing was blocked or dequeued: `POLICY` (the decision was not `ALLOW`; the evaluation is attached), `CONFLICTED` (the rebase onto the destination conflicts; the paths are attached), `UNLANDED_PARENTS` (a parent is not on the destination), `AMENDED` (the change's visible commit is no longer the queued one), `DESTINATION` (the destination bookmark is gone, conflicted, or not local), `BUSY` (the destination moved under three attempts in a row) and `FAILED` (an internal error; the message is attached).

### The gate: `LandChange`

1. Resolve the revision (`@` with a workspace, snapshotted first unless `skip_snapshot`, as reads do); check `expected_commit_id`.
2. Read the change's lifecycle row. `QUEUED` returns its open landing (and waits, below); anything but `PROPOSED` is `FAILED_PRECONDITION`, `NOT_PROPOSED` (metadata `state`, `next`: `reef propose`). A visible commit other than the recorded one is `PROPOSAL_AMENDED` (`next`: `reef propose`): `QUEUED` pins the commit (ADR 0063 decision 6), and landing a commit the change has moved past would leave it divergent.
3. The destination is the proposal's recorded destination. It must be a local bookmark (`DESTINATION_NOT_LOCAL` for `main@origin`, with the hint `jj bookmark track main@origin`), exist (`DESTINATION_NOT_FOUND`) and not be conflicted (`DESTINATION_CONFLICTED`).
4. Run the landing's checks without writing (below: preconditions, the preview, the evaluation). Anything but a clean pass is outcome `BLOCKED` with its reason; the change stays `PROPOSED`, and the landing row records it.
5. Pass: under the create lock shared and the lifecycle lock, check the row is still `PROPOSED` at the same commit, then record **enqueue** (`PROPOSED → QUEUED`, cause `LANDING`, the commit) in an operation of its own (kind `TRANSITION`, `reef: queue change <id>`), with the requester's agent, actor and identity as R3.09 records a request's, and insert the landing row as `QUEUED`. Wake the repository's worker.
6. **Waiting.** `wait_ms` (0 to 600,000; 0 returns once queued) waits for the landing to finish; the response carries the landing as it is when the wait ends. `reef land` waits two minutes by default.

A queue holds at most 1,000 changes per repository (`QUEUE_FULL`, `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`). A blocked request whose landing row would equal the change's latest blocked row (same commit, policy SHA-256, reason and evaluation digest) updates that row's `attempts` and `finished_at` instead of adding one, so retrying a blocked change writes no new row (S-125).

### The queue and its worker

The queue is the `QUEUED` lifecycle rows with an open landing, oldest enqueue first. One worker per repository, started on the first enqueue or at start when queued rows exist, lands them one at a time and exits when the queue is empty. `land_batch(&[Queued]) -> Vec<Outcome>` is the seam: it takes changes in order and lands a prefix, and this ticket calls it with one. R3.14 widens it to a batch with an executor run on the head; R3.21 orders the queue.

### Landing one change

The worker, for the change queued at commit `C` for destination `B`:

1. **Lock.** The repository's create lock shared, held to the end, so GC cannot delete evidence or rewrite operations between the evaluation and the write.
2. **Preview**, on the repository thread, writing nothing: refresh; resolve `B` to its head `D`; check that `C` is still the change's only visible commit (else `AMENDED`), that every parent of `C` is in `::D` (else `UNLANDED_PARENTS`; R3.10 lands stacks), and that `C` has no conflict. If `D` is `C`'s only parent, the landing is a **fast-forward**: the landed commit is `C`. Otherwise **rebase** in memory: the landed tree is `C`'s tree rebased onto `D` (`merge_commit_trees` of `D` with `C`'s diff over its parents). A conflicted result is `CONFLICTED`, with its paths (at most 100, and a total). A clean one **commutes**; the preview also lists the **overlap**, the paths changed both by `C` against its parents and on the destination since them, capped at 1,000 paths compared (more reads as `overlap_total` with the list cut).
3. **Judge.** Build the input for `C` exactly as `EvaluatePolicy` does (the diff against its parents, the evidence on `C`'s tree, the change's provenance), with `change.state = "queued"` and the `landing` object from the preview, read `reef-policy.toml` from `D`'s tree, and evaluate on the blocking pool under an evaluation turn. Anything but `ALLOW` is `POLICY`. The evaluation judges the submitted tree, where the evidence and the sign-offs are (decision 2); the policy is the destination's at the commit the landing moves.
4. **Write**, through `Manager::rewrite` with a new `Write::Land` and the create lock already held (a form of `rewrite` that takes the guard, so the lock is not taken twice): pause, scan-lock and snapshot the workspaces on `C` and its descendants (a snapshot that amends `C` makes the next check fail with `AMENDED`, so what lands is what was judged); take the lifecycle lock; check the row is still `QUEUED` at `C` (otherwise `CANCELLED`: a client moved it); insert the pending transition and the landing's pending rows; then on the repository thread: refresh, check that `B` still points at `D` and that `D`'s policy blob is the one evaluated (otherwise release and go back to step 2, at most three times, then `BUSY`), rebase `C` onto `D` with `rebase_commit` (none for a fast-forward) and `rebase_descendants`, give every jj workspace whose working-copy commit is the landed commit a new empty change on top of it, set `B` to the landed commit, and commit one operation: kind `LAND` (new), description `reef: land change <id> on <bookmark>`, `reef.lifecycle` with `QUEUED → LANDED` (cause `landing`, the landed commit, the destination) and a new attribute `reef.landing` with the landing id.
5. **Commit the rows** in one SQLite transaction: the transition and `lifecycle` (R3.09's step 8), the landing row `LANDED` with its operation, the landed change, its pins (below). Release the lifecycle lock; the workspaces follow (`Manager::rewrite` step 5); publish; wake waiters.
6. **Dequeue** on any refusal at steps 2 to 4: under the lifecycle lock, if the row is still `QUEUED` at `C`, record `QUEUED → PROPOSED` (cause `landing`, the reason in `reason`, kind `TRANSITION`, `reef: dequeue change <id>`), and finish the landing row `DEQUEUED`. The change is the owner's again: it can be fixed, proposed again if it changed, and landed again.

**Workspaces on the landed commit.** An agent often proposes `@`. After a landing, that workspace's working-copy commit is the destination's head; its next snapshot would amend it, and jj would move the bookmark with it, past the gate (S-121). The landing operation therefore gives each such jj workspace a new empty working-copy commit on the landed commit, as `jj new` would, and the Reef workspace follows. Descendant workspaces are rebased with the change, as for any rewrite. R3.09's observed landing has the same hole when someone moves `main` onto a workspace's working-copy commit by hand (#248); this ticket fixes it there too only if the same helper covers it at no extra cost, and says so in its PR.

**Concurrent writers.** Reef's own writes are serialized on the repository thread, so the write's checks see every earlier Reef write. A jj CLI process (or a `git fetch` imported later) can still move `B` between the refresh and the commit; jj then merges the two operations into a conflicted bookmark, visible in the log, which `jj bookmark set` resolves (S-123). The window is the write itself, a few milliseconds.

### What a policy sees: `change.state` and `landing`

Two additions to the input, additive to format 1, each learned by the static check (an older daemon reports a policy naming them `invalid_policy`, ADR 0061's rule):

- **`change.state`**: the change's lifecycle state as text (`draft`, `proposed`, `queued`, …). Inside a landing it is always `queued`; `EvaluatePolicy` reports the state as recorded, so a rule can say "only proposals".
- **`landing`**: `{destination, destination_commit, rebased, overlap, overlap_total}`. In a landing, from the preview. In `EvaluatePolicy`, from the same preview onto the commit the policy is read from (`policy_revision`, `trunk()` by default), so `reef policy eval` answers what a landing would decide now; with policy text instead of a revision, onto `trunk()`. In `SimulatePolicy`, every landing is judged on its own tree: `rebased: false` and no overlap.

With these, a repository decides how much a clean rebase is trusted until R3.14 re-checks the batch head: `[[rule]] name = "fast-forward only", expr = "!landing.rebased"`, or `expr = "size(landing.overlap) == 0 && landing.overlap_total == 0"` for path-disjoint landings. `docs/policy.md` gains both as recipes.

### The structured refusal: rejected evidence

The exit criterion asks that forged or untrusted evidence be refused "with a structured reason", and R3.03 that the reason name the identity status. Today an object that does not count is only counted. This ticket reports, for each missing need of a `[[require]]` rule, the objects on the tree of that kind and target that were present but did not count, and why:

- `reef-policy`: `Need.rejected: [{evidence_id, why}]`, `why` one of `key_unbound`, `key_expired`, `key_revoked`, `key_invalid` (the status at witness time), `untrusted` (no attestor set of the rule accepts its signer), `presence`, `self` (the change's own session or agent), or `condition` (the set's `when` was false), the first that applies, at most 20 per need with a total. Additive to `Evaluation`; the corpus gains cases.
- The daemon: evidence of a needed kind whose signature does not verify never reaches the evaluator; the landing (and `EvaluatePolicy`) adds it to the same list with `why` `signature_invalid` or `signature_unknown_key`, from `SignatureCheck`.
- `PolicyEvidenceNeed` gains `repeated PolicyRejectedEvidence rejected` and `rejected_total`. `reef land` and `reef policy eval` print them under the rule: "missing test from ci: 2 rejected (1 signature_invalid, 1 self)".

### The record

Migration `0013_landings.sql` (schema 13, renumbered at rebase if another ticket takes 13):

```sql
CREATE TABLE landings(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, id TEXT NOT NULL,          -- lnd_<16 hex>
             status TEXT NOT NULL,                       -- pending | committed
             destination TEXT NOT NULL,
             destination_before TEXT, destination_after TEXT,    -- commit ids
             outcome TEXT NOT NULL,                      -- blocked | queued | landed | dequeued | cancelled
             reason TEXT, message TEXT,
             policy_commit TEXT, policy_blob TEXT, policy_sha256 TEXT,
             attempts INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,
             enqueue_operation_id TEXT, operation_id TEXT,      -- the land or dequeue
             operation_collected INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
             agent TEXT, actor TEXT, identity_id TEXT, remote_token TEXT,
             created_at INTEGER NOT NULL, finished_at INTEGER,
             PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, id)) WITHOUT ROWID;
CREATE TABLE landing_changes(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, landing_id TEXT NOT NULL, position INTEGER NOT NULL,
             change_id TEXT NOT NULL, queued_commit TEXT NOT NULL, queued_tree TEXT NOT NULL,
             landed_commit TEXT, landed_tree TEXT, rebased INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
             overlap TEXT,                               -- JSON list, at most 100, and the total
             conflicts TEXT,                             -- JSON list, at most 100, and the total
             decision TEXT, evaluation TEXT,             -- reef_policy::Evaluation as JSON
             evaluation_sha256 TEXT,
             PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, landing_id, position)) WITHOUT ROWID;
CREATE TABLE landing_evidence(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, landing_id TEXT NOT NULL,
             change_id TEXT NOT NULL, evidence_id TEXT NOT NULL,
             PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, evidence_id, landing_id)) WITHOUT ROWID;
CREATE TABLE landing_policies(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, sha256 TEXT NOT NULL, bytes BLOB NOT NULL,
             first_used_at INTEGER NOT NULL,
             PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, sha256)) WITHOUT ROWID;
-- indexes: landings by (repo_id, created_at DESC), (repo_id, destination, outcome, finished_at DESC),
-- (status) WHERE status = 'pending'; landing_changes by (repo_id, change_id)
```

- **One row per attempt**, written in R3.09's order: pending rows (with the pending transition), the operation, then committed in one SQLite transaction. Start-up resolution (R3.09's `resolve_pending`) commits a pending landing with the operation it finds by the transition id, or drops it; a `QUEUED` change whose landing was dropped is landed again by the worker.
- **Every landed or dequeued change keeps its evaluation** (JSON, bounded by the policy's size), its decision and the evaluation's SHA-256, so a reader sees exactly what the gate saw, and R3.08 can judge a landing again with a proposed policy and the pinned evidence (#246).
- **`repo forget`** deletes all four tables by repository; backups copy them whole (ADR 0020); GC maps `enqueue_operation_id` and `operation_id` through its rewrite as it does lifecycle rows, and marks dropped ones `operation_collected`. GC never deletes landing rows.

### Pins (decision 5)

- **Evidence**: every `evidence_ids` entry of every satisfied rule of a landed change's evaluation gets a `landing_evidence` row in the landing's commit transaction, and an `evidence_links` row to the change (R3.05), so `ListEvidence(change_id)` shows it. `PINNED` gains `OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM landing_evidence p WHERE p.repo_id = e.repo_id AND p.evidence_id = e.id)`. Pinned evidence is kept until `repo forget`.
- **Artifacts** named by landing-pinned evidence keep their own retention: the artifact plan reads a variant of the condition without the landing clause, so a landed check's log expires after `[artifacts] retention` and reads as collected, while the evidence that names it stays.
- **The policy**: the bytes the evaluation ran are copied into `landing_policies` by SHA-256 on the first landing that used them. The blob in git stays reachable from the destination's history, but a force-moved or rewritten destination could let `git gc` collect it; the copy cannot be.
- **Provenance**: the records of a landed change that its evaluation read are exempt from compaction and expiry (`provenance_to_compact` and `provenance_to_expire` skip changes with a landing), so the provenance a landing judged stays whole.

### Undo, observed landings, GC (decision 6)

- **Not undoable**: `UndoOperation` of an enqueue, a dequeue or a land is `OPERATION_NOT_UNDOABLE`. A land: "a landing moved <bookmark>; land a revert, or move the bookmark back with jj"; an enqueue: "withdraw the proposal instead"; a dequeue: "land it again". ADR 0063 decision 10 already refuses an operation that changed the view and carries transitions; the lifecycle undo path refuses cause `landing`.
- **Observed landings** see a landed change as `LANDED` and skip it (`record_observed` re-reads under the lifecycle lock, which the landing holds until its rows commit). A queued change someone lands by hand while it waits is recorded `observed`; the worker then finds the row `LANDED` and finishes the landing `CANCELLED`.
- **GC**: the id-mapping step covers `landings`, beside `lifecycle_transitions`.

## Implementation notes

- **Order**: reef-lib's preview and land (test 1); reef-policy's fields and rejected evidence (2); the input builder moved out of the handler (existing policy tests stay green); the migration and store; the gate and a synchronous land with no queue (3, 4, 12); the queue and worker (8); rebases (5); in-flight races and workspaces (6, 9); pins and GC (10, 13); undo (11); the API surface, remote rule and preview (14, 15); the CLI, schema and MCP (16); the web UI; the documents.
- **Locks**: create (shared) for the whole landing, then the lifecycle lock from the final row check to the rows' commit; never the create lock twice (`Manager::rewrite` takes the held guard). The evaluation runs outside the lifecycle lock, so client transitions on other changes are not held behind CEL.
- **Threads**: preview and write are repository-thread jobs; evaluation on the blocking pool with a turn; SQLite on the async side's blocking pool.
- **Linux**: nothing is `cfg(target_os = "linux")`, but the daemon's Linux build and clippy run in the privileged container before pushing.
- **No new configuration.** The queue cap, the retries, the caps on paths and rejected objects are constants.

## Risks

- **A clean rebase is not a test** (S-120). Mitigated by the overlap in the record and the input, the recipes, and R3.14.
- **A local `main` that also follows a git host** diverges from it after a landing, and a later fetch can conflict the bookmark. Mitigated by landing only on request and only with a policy, and by `docs/landing.md`; R3.17 pushes.
- **Moving the policy input out of the handler** touches R3.07's and R3.08's code; their tests are the guard, and nothing else is changing it in this round.
- **`Manager::rewrite` with a held lock** is a change to the write path every client write takes; the existing write and lifecycle tests run unchanged.
- **Field numbers** in `operations.proto` and `policy.proto` may collide with R3.25's or R3.42's at rebase; renumbered then.

## Choices this brief made, and why

- **A landing per attempt, beside the lifecycle rows**, because a landing carries an evaluation, a policy, pins and two operations that a transition row has no place for, and because blocked attempts never become transitions.
- **Blocked is a response, not an error**, because the structured refusal is the evaluation itself, which does not fit `ErrorDetail`.
- **The policy at the commit the landing moves, checked again in the write**, because R3.07 made the destination's policy the one in force, and a policy that changed while the landing waited must judge it.
- **New working-copy commits on the landed commit**, because otherwise the next snapshot rewrites the destination's head and jj moves the bookmark past the gate.
- **Any `write` token may request a landing**, because proposing is the request and the policy decides; who asked is recorded.
- **No git export**, because no Reef write exports today and the jj CLI does it on its next command; R3.17 pushes landed history.
- **The evaluation outside the lifecycle lock, re-checked inside**, so a slow policy holds back no other change's transitions.

## Decisions for the user

Asked on 2026-11-08; the user accepted every recommendation below, and the brief's body follows them. Decision 1's new row is R3.45.

**Group 1: scope**

1. **Split.** R3.13 lands on request (`LandChange`, `reef land`) through the queue, with everything else in this brief; a new row (R3.45, M, depends on R3.13 and R3.05), "Automatic landing and evidence requests", re-evaluates open proposals when their evidence, provenance or the destination's policy changes, queues those policy allows when the destination's policy opts in (`[landing] automatic = true`), and submits jobs for missing evidence. *Recommended*: split. Alternatives: everything in R3.13 (well past L); or evidence requests in R3.14, which runs the executor anyway, and automatic landing in the new row.

**Group 2: what lands, on what evidence**

2. **What is judged, and what a rebase must satisfy.** The queued commit (the submitted tree, where the evidence and sign-offs are) is judged with the destination's policy at the commit the landing moves; when the destination moved, the change is rebased and lands if the rebase is clean; the overlap is recorded and given to policy as `landing.rebased` and `landing.overlap`, so a repository can require fast-forward or disjoint paths; R3.14 then tests the batch head. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: fast-forward only until R3.14 (the landed tree is always the tested one, but every moved `main` means rebase, re-run and re-propose); or judge the rebased tree (evidence and sign-offs must be on the landed tree, so most landings need new evidence and a new sign-off until R3.15).
3. **Parents and conflicts.** A change lands only when all its parents are on the destination (`UNLANDED_PARENTS`; stacks are R3.10); a conflicting rebase writes nothing and dequeues the change with the paths, for its owner to rebase (conflict objects come with R3.25 and R3.11). *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: rebase the change alone (`jj rebase -r`), leaving its parents behind; or leave the conflicted rebase in the change for its owner.
4. **Destinations and workspaces.** Local bookmarks only (`main@origin` refused, with a hint to track it); no git export (the jj CLI exports on its next command, as for every Reef write); every workspace on the landed commit gets a new empty change on top in the landing's operation, so later edits cannot move `main`. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: also export the destination bookmark to git in a colocated repository in the same operation.

**Group 3: records, pins, undo**

5. **Pins.** The evidence that satisfied a landed change's rules is pinned until `repo forget` and linked to the change; the policy bytes are copied into `reef.sqlite3` by SHA-256; the change's provenance records are exempt from compaction; artifacts named by pinned evidence keep `[artifacts] retention` and become tombstones. *Recommended*: as stated (evidence is 1 to 2 KiB, logs can be large). Alternatives: pinned evidence also pins its artifacts for good; or pins expire after a long retention (say two years).
6. **Undo.** Enqueue, dequeue and land are not undoable (`OPERATION_NOT_UNDOABLE`, with "land a revert" or "withdraw" as hints). *Recommended*: as stated, since others build on `main` at once and `LANDED` is final. Alternative: a land is undoable while the destination has not moved since (the view reverted and the change back to `PROPOSED`, so `LANDED` stops being final).

**Group 4: the surface**

7. **API.** A new `LandingService` (`LandChange` WRITE, waiting up to `wait_ms`; `GetLanding`, `ListLandings` READ), landing records with outcomes and reasons, `OPERATION_KIND_LAND`, `TRANSITION_CAUSE_LANDING`, `Operation.landing_id`, rejected evidence on `PolicyEvidenceNeed`; any `write` token may request a landing; API revision 23. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: the RPCs on `ChangeService` beside `TransitionChange`; or `LandChange` returning at once, always, with clients following the operation stream.
8. **CLI, MCP, help.** `reef land` and `reef landing list|show` in `--help-all`; `reef propose`'s `next` line points at `reef land`; the three RPCs in `NOT_TOOLS`; root help and agent guide unchanged. *Recommended*: as stated (`tools/list` is at its budget, #116; the new row is when proposing becomes the default path). Alternative: MCP tools `propose_change` and `land_change` (about 1.4 KB more in `tools/list`, raising the budget) and a guide line "reef propose, then reef land", dropping another line to fit 1,600 bytes.
