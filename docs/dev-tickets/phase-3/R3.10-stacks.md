# R3.10 Stacks

Phase 3 · Size M, at its top end (decision 2) · Depends on: R3.09 (the lifecycle, `TransitionChange`, several transitions in one operation), and builds on R3.13 (`LandChange`, the gate, landing rows), R3.14 (batches, chains, bisect over prefixes), R3.16 (the projection: one commit per change) and R3.17 (a landing's push) · Unblocks: R3.11 (dependent change lifecycle), R3.24 (a `stack` policy input built from these edges), #268 (`reef publish --stack`)

## Context

The design's data model gives a change "dependency edges on other changes, so a stack of changes is explicit", and its Landing section says "an agent proposes a change plus evidence, or a stack of dependent changes as one proposal", and "a stack lands as a prefix: if a later change fails, the earlier ones still land". Its human interface reviews "stacks, not commits": "the reviewer accepts the proposal or a prefix of its stack". Phase 3's exit criteria include "a stack lands as a prefix when a later change fails".

Every earlier ticket left this ticket a seam:

- **R3.09**: "A change whose ancestors are unlanded drafts may be proposed; whether its ancestors must be proposed with it is R3.10's", and `reef.lifecycle` holds a list "so that R3.10 records a stack's transitions in one operation".
- **R3.13**: "A change whose parents have not landed is refused (`unlanded_parents`) … R3.10 lands stacks", and "R3.10 lands a stack as one landing with several `landing_changes` and several transitions in one operation, landing a prefix".
- **R3.14**: "A member's parents must be on `D` … R3.10 lets a member's parents be earlier members", and its bisect already lands the passing prefix of a chain.
- **R3.16**: "A stack (R3.10) is where several changes become one commit; R3.10 adds `squash = "stack"` to `[history]`" (decision 7 declines this).
- **R3.17**: "R3.10 pushes a stack's squashed commit like any other landing."
- **R3.24** writes policy rules "over a `stack` input built from R3.10's dependency edges"; **#268** publishes a stack as stacked review branches.

The direction since 2026-11-09: agents are the users, git and jj compatibility stays whole, and nothing is hooked further into git. An agent that splits a task into three changes should propose and land them with the two commands it already uses, and see per change what landed and why the rest did not.

So this ticket builds:

1. **The stack**, derived from jj's parent edges, never stored (decision 1).
2. **Proposing, withdrawing and declining a stack** as one operation (decision 3).
3. **Landing a stack**: `reef land <head>` lands the head with its proposed ancestors, the passing prefix first, in one land operation (decisions 4 and 6).
4. **Reviewing a stack**: a sign-off on a member's tree counts for the members below it (decision 5).
5. **The record and the surface**: schema 21, API revision 35, the CLI's stack forms, and the MCP tools `propose_change` and `land_change` (decision 8).

- **Several transitions per operation** already exist: `OpMeta.transitions` is a list (`reef-lib/src/jj/oplog.rs:474`), a batch's land writes one per member, and `undo_inner` (`lifecycle/mod.rs:1164`) inverts every transition of an operation in one undo. ### What a stack is (decision 1)

**The edges are jj's parent edges.** A change depends on the changes of its commit's parents. jj already stores those edges on every commit, rewrites them on every rebase, and keeps change ids through it; `Change.parent_change_ids` reports them. A stored edge table would be a second copy that every rebase, squash and `jj` CLI command can make wrong, and a change still cannot land without its parent commit, so the graph would win anyway.

**The stack of a change `C` for destination `B`** is `(::C) ~ (::D)`, where `D` is `B`'s head: `C` and every ancestor not yet on the destination. Its members are in topological order, parents first (jj's order, reversed), and its **bottom** is the first.

- **Bounded**: at most 32 members (`MAX_STACK`, R3.14's largest batch), else `STACK_TOO_LARGE` (`FAILED_PRECONDITION`, metadata `size`, `max`).
- **Merges allowed.** A member with two parents in the stack lands as R3.14 lands any member, rebased onto the tip with its diff over its parents, so the landed history is linear. A merge whose other parent is on `D` is a member like any other.
- **Nothing is recorded as "a stack".** Proposals are per change (R3.09); the stack is read from the graph whenever it is needed. A landing records which request a member was landed by (`stack`, below) for the record, never to decide anything.
- **A recorded change's commit is its visible one**: an amended member is the member, and propose and land treat it as R3.09 and R3.13 do (`amended`, `PROPOSAL_AMENDED`).

A single change on the destination is a stack of one, so every existing call keeps its behavior.

### Proposing a stack (decision 3)

`TransitionChangeRequest.stack` (new): the transition applies to every member of the revision's stack, in one operation.

- **`propose` with `stack`**: the destination is resolved as R3.09 resolves it (the request's, else the head's recorded one, else `trunk()`'s), and every member that is not already `PROPOSED` at its visible commit for that destination is proposed (from `DRAFT`, `WITHDRAWN` or `DECLINED`, or an update of a `PROPOSED` member that was amended or had another destination). `QUEUED` members are left as they are. One operation, kind `transition`, described `reef: propose stack of 3 changes (head <id>)`, with one transition per member written, bottom first. A member whose transition would be refused refuses the whole request, with R3.09's reason and metadata naming that member (`CHANGE_EMPTY` with hint "abandon it: `reef abandon <id>`", `CHANGE_CONFLICTED`, `CHANGE_DIVERGENT`, `TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` for a `LANDED` or `SUPERSEDED` member). Nothing to do is a no-op, as R3.09's.
- **`withdraw` and `decline` with `stack`**: every `PROPOSED` or `QUEUED` member, in one operation; others are skipped. `supersede` with `stack` is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, `STACK_NOT_ALLOWED`.
- **Without `stack`** the request is R3.09's, unchanged: a change on unproposed ancestors can be proposed alone; it cannot land until they are proposed (below). R3.11 makes such a change a linked dependent proposal.
- **Remote listener**: `Rule::Lifecycle` is checked per member, and one member another token proposed refuses the whole request (`REMOTE_PROPOSAL_NOT_OWNED`, naming it). The lifecycle lock is held across all members.
- **Undo** of the operation inverts every member's transition in one undo (R3.09's path, unchanged).
- **CLI**: `reef propose [REV]` proposes the stack by default, and `--only` proposes the revision alone; `reef proposal withdraw|decline [REV] --stack`. The output lists each member (change, state, round) bottom first, then the operation.

### Landing a stack (decision 4)

`reef land C` (`LandChange` on `C`) lands `C`'s stack: a change cannot land without its parents, so landing `C` is landing every member below it. No new request field.

**The gate**, in place of R3.13's single-change gate:

1. Resolve `C` and its stack onto the head's recorded destination `D`; `STACK_TOO_LARGE` past 32.
2. **Every member must be a proposal for the same destination**: `PROPOSED` at its visible commit, or `QUEUED`. Otherwise a request error naming the member, as R3.13's are for the head: `NOT_PROPOSED` (metadata `change_id`, `state`; `next` `reef propose <C>`, which proposes the stack), `PROPOSAL_AMENDED` (`next` `reef propose <C>`), or the new `STACK_DESTINATION_MISMATCH` (metadata `change_id`, `destination`; `next` `reef propose <C> --to <B>`). A head already `QUEUED` with every member queued returns its open landings, as R3.13 does.
3. **Judge the members in order, as a chain.** `QUEUED` members (an earlier request queued them) stay in the chain unjudged; formation judges them again anyway. Each other member is judged as R3.13 judges a change, with the preview of the chain up to it: its parents may be `D`'s ancestors or earlier members' queued commits, and its `landing` input is the chain's (R3.14's overlap with earlier members). Sign-offs from above count (decision 5).
4. **The prefix.** The members before the first that does not pass are queued, in one operation (kind `transition`, `reef: queue 3 changes (stack of 5, head <id>)`, one enqueue transition per member). The first that does not pass is `BLOCKED` with its reason, and every member above it is `BLOCKED` with `UNLANDED_PARENTS` and a message naming it ("<id> below it was blocked: policy"). Nothing is queued when the bottom does not pass.
5. **Policy files split a stack.** A member that changes `reef-policy.toml` or `reef-checks.toml` must be judged before anything above it lands (S-146), and R3.14 lands it as the first member of a batch. At the gate it ends the prefix: at the bottom it is queued alone, elsewhere the members below it are queued and it is blocked with the new reason **`ALONE`** ("it changes reef-policy.toml, which lands on its own: land the changes below it, then `reef land <id>`"); the members above are blocked `UNLANDED_PARENTS`. `[landing] alone` paths do not split a stack (decision 6).
6. **Rows.** Each member gets its own landing row, as R3.13's (blocked rows deduplicated by R3.13's rule), each carrying `stack`, the landing id of the head's row in this request. The response's `landing` is the head's; `stack` lists the others, bottom first.
7. **Waiting**: `wait_ms` waits until every queued member's landing has finished.

**Why a blocked prefix is not all or nothing**: the design asks for prefix landing, and a stack's lower members are complete changes their owner chose to put first. A request that wants nothing landed unless the head lands withdraws, fixes and re-proposes; `--atomic` is not built.

### Batches (decision 6)

A stack must land in one land operation, because landing a member rewrites the members above it (Context). Formation therefore works on **stack groups**:

- **A group** is an open landing and every open landing for the same destination whose queued commit descends from it, bottom first, read once per formation from the queued commits' parents on the repository thread. A queued change with no queued ancestor is a group of one, so batches of unrelated changes are R3.14's.
- **A group joins a batch whole or not at all.** It may make the batch larger than `batch_max` only when it is the batch's first group (at most 32 members, the gate's bound). It never starts mid-batch past `batch_max`.
- **Judging a group member** previews the group's chain up to it (the gate's step 3). A member that is blocked, excluded from the chain (conflicting with an earlier member, or emptied by one), amended, or refused at landing is dequeued with its reason, and **its queued descendants in the group are dequeued with it**, `UNLANDED_PARENTS` naming it, in the same operation. R3.14 left an excluded member queued for the next batch; a group member cannot wait, because the landing of the members below it rewrites it.
- **Bisect**: the culprit's queued descendants are dequeued `UNLANDED_PARENTS` ("<id> below it failed checks") rather than requeued; other members above the culprit are requeued as R3.14 does.
- **`[landing] alone`**: a group with an `alone` member is a batch of its own: no other group joins it, and the stack still lands as one. `alone` keeps unrelated work out of a risky landing; splitting a stack would strand its upper half. Policy-file members never reach formation above another member (the gate's step 5).
- **The trial and `batch` input** are R3.14's: a group is members like any other, and `batch.position` counts them.

One helper owns "dequeue this member and its queued descendants", used by formation, the landing's re-judgment, `refused`, bisect and the gate.

### Reviewing a stack (decision 5)

A reviewer reads a stack as one diff, `D` to the head, and "accepts the proposal or a prefix of its stack". R3.07's sign-off is evidence on one tree. So: **a sign-off on a member's submitted tree counts for every member below it in the stack being landed.** Signing the head accepts the stack; signing member *k* accepts the prefix to *k*, and the gate lands that prefix and blocks the rest for want of a sign-off.

- Only sign-off kinds: the kinds any `[[signoff]]` names, and `signoff` for `builtin:owners`. Tests and other `[[require]]` evidence stay on the member's own tree (and on R3.14's tested trees), since a head that passes says nothing about a prefix that does not build.
- The daemon adds those objects, each verified as today, to member *j*'s `evidence` input from the submitted trees of the members above *j* in the gate's stack or the batch's group; the evaluator is unchanged. A pinned landing pins them too (`landing_evidence`), so `reef landing show` names the sign-off that let each member land.
- A member's tree is a prefix of the head's: the head's tree contains every member below it as queued, so an amended lower member rebases the head and takes the sign-off away (R3.15 owns carrying it).
- `reef evidence add --kind signoff` on the head, unchanged, is how a person accepts the stack; R3.31 adds the button.

### History (decision 7)

**One commit per change**, R3.16's unit, for stacks too. A stack lands as one commit per member, in order, each with its change id, projected by `[history]` like any batch. `squash = "stack"` is not built: the members' change ids would vanish from the destination, which the history index reads as abandoned (`follow.rs:345`), and `reef show`, per-change bisect and revert, and R3.11's dependents would all need a squashed-into map. With prefix landing, "the stack" is not one unit at landing time either: a squashed prefix and a later squashed remainder would be two commits whose split nobody chose. R3.16's trailers name the landing (`Reef-Landing`, and `Reef-Batch` for several changes), which already groups a stack's commits on the host.

### The CLI (decision 8)

```
reef propose [REV] [--only] [-m TEXT] [--to BOOKMARK] …   # the stack by default
reef proposal withdraw|decline [REV] [--stack] [-m TEXT]
reef proposal show [REV]                                  # gains the stack: each member, its state, bottom first
```

- **Help**: `--help-all` only, as now; root help and guide unchanged.
- **Web UI**: `words.ts` names a multi-member transition "proposed a stack of 3 changes" and a multi-member enqueue "queued 3 changes"; nothing else.

### MCP (decision 8)

Two tools, as small as the schema allows, so an MCP agent proposes and lands without leaving Reef for a shell:

| Tool | RPC | Arguments | Waits | Returns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `propose_change` | `TransitionChange`, `to` `PROPOSED`, `stack` unless `only` | `repo`, `workspace`, `revision` (default `@`), `only` | No: a proposal is recorded at once. | the brief `Transition`: each member's change, state and round, and the operation |
| `land_change` | `LandChange` | `repo`, `workspace`, `revision` (default `@`) | Yes, up to two minutes, `reef land`'s default: an agent's next step depends on the outcome, as `push_landed` waits for its push. A landing still queued then returns `outcome: queued` with `next`; calling the tool again returns the same open landing and waits again. | the brief landing of the head and each other member: `id`, `outcome`, `reason`, the unsatisfied rules' names, and `next` |

- Annotations: `propose_change` is not destructive and is idempotent (an unchanged proposal writes nothing, R3.09); `land_change` is not destructive and not idempotent.
- A blocked or dequeued landing is a result, not a tool error, as for `push_landed`'s states: the agent reads `reason` and `next`.
- `crates/reef-vcs/tests/mcp.rs` raises the `tools/list` bound by what the two tools measure, with the argument in its comment: without them an MCP agent has no way to put its work forward or land it, and leaves Reef for `reef propose` and `reef land` in a shell at the root.
- `mcp/instructions.md`: "propose_change puts a change and the changes below it forward; land_change lands them" joins the existing proposing sentence.

### Threat model rows

- **S-207 Landing another agent's proposal through a stack.** `reef land C` lands proposed ancestors someone else proposed. Accepted and recorded: they are proposals, each is judged by policy, and any `write` caller may already request a landing (R3.13); the landing rows name the requester.
- **S-208 Proposing another's draft through a stack.** `reef propose` on a head proposes ancestors another agent is still writing. Recorded with the proposer; the output names each member; `--only` exists; through the remote listener a member another token proposed refuses the request.
- **S-209 A sign-off reaching further than its reviewer meant.** A head's sign-off accepts every member below. Documented: accepting a prefix is signing its top; a lower member's later amendment changes the head's tree and voids it.
- **S-210 A policy change inside a stack.** Members above a policy-file change would be judged by the old policy. Fixed: the gate ends the prefix at it (`ALONE`).
- **S-211 Work by size.** A 32-member bound on stacks; a group is judged once per formation; one parent read per formation.
- **S-212 A stack split across operations.** A landed prefix rewrites the members above it. Fixed: groups land in one operation, and the members a landing leaves behind are returned in the same step, never left queued at a commit that no longer exists.
- **S-213 Undo of a stack's transitions.** One undo reverts every member's client transition, refused as R3.09 refuses when any is no longer its change's latest.

3. `propose_stack`: one operation with three transitions; `--only` semantics without `stack`; an empty middle member refuses the request naming it; undo reverts all three; a remote token refused for a member another token proposed.
4. `land_stack_whole`: three members land in one `land` operation, one commit each with its change id, `stack` set on every row, `GetLanding` lists them.
5. `gate_prefix`: the middle member lacks a sign-off; the bottom lands, the middle is `BLOCKED` `POLICY`, the head `BLOCKED` `UNLANDED_PARENTS` naming it; exit 5 from `reef land` with the landed member printed.
6. `signoff_on_head_accepts_stack`: a `[[signoff]]` rule; one sign-off on the head's tree lands all three; one on the middle's tree lands two.
7. `bisect_prefix` (`checks = "batch"`, a scripted executor failing on the middle member's tree): the bottom lands, the middle is dequeued `checks`, the head dequeued `UNLANDED_PARENTS`, an unrelated change in the same batch above them requeued and landed next. This is Phase 3's exit criterion.
8. `group_is_whole`: `batch_max = 2` and a three-member stack queued behind one other change: the other change lands alone, then the stack lands in one batch of three.
9. `policy_file_member`: a stack whose middle member edits `reef-policy.toml`: the bottom queued, the middle `ALONE`, the head `UNLANDED_PARENTS`.
10. `not_proposed_member` and `destination_mismatch`: request errors naming the member, with `next`.
11. `withdrawn_member_while_queued`: withdrawing the middle of a queued stack dequeues the head with it at the next formation; the bottom lands.

## Risks

- **Formation's change is the riskiest part**: R3.14's formation is long and branches on many refusals. Mitigated by one helper for "dequeue with dependents" and by groups of one reproducing today's batches exactly (R3.14's tests run unchanged).
- **Proposing by default widens what a bare `reef propose` does.** Mitigated by the per-member output and `--only`; on one change it is unchanged.
- **Prefix landing strands the upper members as amended** after a rebased or summarized prefix lands. They are returned with a reason and are proposed again with one command; R3.11 carries them.
- **Numbers** collide with #263's at rebase; renumbered then.

## Choices this brief made, and why

- **Edges from jj's graph**, because jj already keeps them right through every rewrite, and a stored copy could only disagree with the commit that lands.
- **No stack object**, because proposals are the change's (R3.09), and the graph says what a stack is at the moment it matters.
- **Landing a head lands its ancestors**, because a change cannot land without its parents, and every member is a proposal judged on its own.
- **Groups land whole**, because a landing rewrites the members above it.
- **Sign-offs reach down, tests do not**, because a reviewer reads the stack's whole diff, while a head's passing tests say nothing about a prefix.
- **One commit per change**, because the change id is how every other part of Reef finds work.

## What later tickets inherit

- **R3.11** carries proposals of members left behind across the landing's rewrite, orders separately proposed dependents by these edges, and returns dependents of a declined or withdrawn member with conflict objects.
- **R3.24** builds the `stack` policy input from `land::stack` and the gate's and formation's chains.
- **#268** publishes `land::stack`'s members as stacked review branches.
- **R3.31** shows a proposal's stack from `reef proposal show`'s read and signs the head to accept it.

## Departures from dev-phases.md

`dev-phases.md` and this directory's README were edited to match with this brief:

- **"Dependency edges on changes"** are jj's parent edges, read from the graph, not stored. A stack is a change and its ancestors not yet on the destination.
- **"Propose, review, and land a stack as one unit"**: one operation proposes, withdraws or declines a stack; a sign-off on a member accepts the members below it; `reef land` on the head lands the stack in one land operation.
- **"With prefix landing"**: at the gate and in a batch's bisect; a policy-file change ends the prefix (`ALONE`).
- **R3.16's row** no longer says stacks squash with R3.10: a stack lands one commit per change (decision 7).

## Decisions for the user

**Answered 2026-11-18**: the user accepted 1 to 7 as recommended; 8 adds the MCP tools `propose_change` and `land_change`.

**Group 1: shape and scope**

1. **Edges and the stack.** The dependency edges are jj's parent edges, read from the graph; a stack is a change and its ancestors not on the destination (`D..C`), topological, merges allowed, at most 32; nothing stores a stack. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: a stored edge table (`change_dependencies`) that can name dependencies the graph does not have, reconciled with the graph at landing; or a stored stack object (`stk_…`) with ordered members recorded at proposal.
2. **Size.** M at its top end; the `stack` policy input goes to R3.24 and `EvaluatePolicy` over a stack to a follow-up. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: add the `stack` input here (`{size, position, changes}`, about 150 lines, touching reef-policy's static check), leaving R3.24 only rules.

**Group 2: behavior**

3. **Proposing.** `reef propose` proposes the stack by default (one operation), `--only` the change alone; the RPC's `stack` field is off by default; `withdraw` and `decline` take `--stack`. *Recommended*: as stated (one command for an agent's multi-change task; unchanged for one change). Alternatives: `--stack` opt-in on propose too; or refuse a lone propose on unproposed ancestors (`UNPROPOSED_PARENTS`, `next` `reef propose --stack`).
4. **Landing.** `reef land <head>` lands the head with its proposed ancestors; every member must be proposed for the same destination (else a request error naming it); the passing prefix is queued and the rest blocked, the first with its reason and those above `unlanded_parents`. *Recommended*: as stated (the design's prefix landing). Alternatives: all or nothing (block the whole stack when any member fails); or land only with a `--stack` flag and keep `unlanded_parents` otherwise.
5. **Review.** A sign-off on a member's tree counts for every member below it in the stack being landed; tests do not. *Recommended*: as stated ("accepts the proposal or a prefix of its stack" is signing its top). Alternatives: each member signed on its own tree, with `reef evidence add --stack` signing each; or every evidence kind on the head counts for the members below (tests too).
6. **Batches.** A stack lands in one land operation: its queued members form a group that joins a batch whole, may exceed `batch_max` when first (up to 32), forms a batch of its own when a member touches an `alone` path; a policy-file member ends the prefix at the gate (new reason `alone`); a returned member takes its queued descendants with it. *Recommended*: as stated (a landed prefix rewrites the members above it). Alternatives: split groups at `batch_max` and at `alone` paths, returning the stranded upper members `amended`; or a stack never batches with other changes.
7. **History.** One commit per change for stacks too; `squash = "stack"` is not built, and R3.16's row is edited. *Recommended*: as stated (change ids stay visible; the history index, bisect, revert and R3.11 keep working; prefix landing makes "the stack" two units anyway). Alternative: build `squash = "stack"` (the landed prefix as one commit with the head's change id, members recorded on the landing rows and read as landed by the history index; L).
