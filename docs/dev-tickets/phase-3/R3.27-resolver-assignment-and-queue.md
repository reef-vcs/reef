# R3.27 Resolver assignment and queue

Phase 3 · Size M, at its top end, with the scope lines below · Depends on: R3.25 (`reef_lib::jj::conflict::read`, conflict and file ids, `introduced`, `ConflictService`, `reef conflict`), and builds on R3.03 (identities, sessions, actors), R3.04 (provenance records found by change, provenance sessions and their `ended_at`, the declared client session), R3.44 (client sessions declared by harnesses), R2.09 (the operation stream and its filters), R2.13 and R2.26 (how agents hold and resume workspaces), R2.08 (remote tokens and `remote/rules.rs`), R3.09 (operations that carry Reef's own records, the observer task, `identity_for`) · Unblocks: R3.28; through it R3.29 and R3.30, and the conflict parts of R3.33 and R3.35

## Context

The design names the job in two places. Data model, Conflicts: "A conflicted snapshot is a legal parent, can be pushed, and can be assigned to another change for resolution. Conflicts also carry backpressure: landing refuses a conflicted change, a conflict whose owner's session has ended moves to a resolver queue with a priority, and a change cannot acquire a second unresolved conflict ancestor without policy approval." Human interface: "A conflict object is assigned automatically to a resolver, by default the originating agent of the later change, which sees base, both sides, and the task and instruction behind each … A human can still take a conflict over and resolve it by hand, but that is the exception." The fleet dashboard lists "the conflict queue", and the triage inbox's needs-you-now bucket holds "a conflict nobody owns".

R3.25 left this ticket the object and a warning. The object: one per conflicted commit, derived from jj on every read and never stored, with a conflict id (`cfl_…`, over the change id and its files' ids) that changes when a conflicted path's sides change, and `introduced`, the files no parent carries. The warning, in `docs/conflicts.md`: "Route work on `verified` sources only: R3.27's resolver assignment does." And in its brief: "R3.27 … keys its rows by conflict id or file id and re-reads before acting."

Since nothing about a conflict is stored, everything this ticket decides is new state that only Reef knows: who owns each conflict, whether it waits in a queue, who claimed it, and what became of it. That state has to survive the rewrites that keep changing the conflict's commit (and, often, its id) under it.

So this ticket builds:

1. **The assignment**: one row per conflicted change that introduces a conflict, keyed by the change, opened, updated and closed by a daemon task that follows the operation log, with an owner computed from R3.04's provenance.
2. **Liveness**: what "the owner's session has ended" means observably, how an assignment follows an agent that moved to another workspace, and when it moves to the queue.
3. **The queue**: per repository, ordered by priority then age, from which resolver agents claim; a claim lives as long as the claiming workspace's session.
4. **People**: reassignment to a workspace or the queue, priority, and manual takeover.
5. **The surface**: five RPCs on `ConflictService`, an assignment on every `Conflict`, a workspace's assignments in `GetStatus`, an operation per change of state, `reef conflict queue|assignments|claim|release|assign|take`, and the documents.

Tiers and attempt caps (R3.28), intent escalation (R3.29), backpressure (R3.30), the conflict view (R3.35) and conflicts jj does not store (R3.26, R3.11) are out of scope, each with the seam it needs ("Out of scope").

### What is assigned, and its key (decisions 1 and 3)

An **assignment** is Reef's record that one change carries a conflict someone must resolve, and who that is. It is keyed by **(repository, change id, kind)**, with at most one open assignment per key.

- **Per change, not per conflict id.** The conflict id changes whenever a conflicted path's sides change: a partial resolution (two of three files fixed), a re-rebase onto a moved destination, a describe that is not, but an edit to a conflicted file is. An assignment keyed by it would be closed and reopened by the resolver's own progress, losing its owner and its claims. The change id survives every rewrite jj does, and the resolver's job is "make this change conflict-free". The row keeps `conflict_id` (as last read), `first_conflict_id` and `conflict_changed_at`, so a consumer still sees that the conflict moved under it (S-132), and `ClaimConflict` and `AssignConflict` take an `expected_conflict_id`.
- **`kind`** is R3.25's `ConflictKind`: `TREE` now. R3.26's `UNDONE_OPERATION` and R3.11's `MISSING_DEPENDENCY` open their own assignments for the same change through the same table, so a change with a tree conflict and an undone dependency has two.
- **Only introduced conflicts.** A commit whose every conflicted file is `inherited` from a parent (R3.25) gets no assignment: its parent's assignment covers it, and resolving the parent lets jj rebase the child clean. A child that also introduces a conflict of its own gets its own.
- **Only `mutable()` and visible.** The follower reads `mutable() & conflicts()` at the head, as `ListConflicts` does. A divergent change (two visible conflicted commits) has one assignment, tracking the newer commit in log order.
- **One episode at a time.** When the change stops carrying the conflict the assignment closes (decision 8). If the same conflict id comes back within 24 hours (an undo of the resolution, a rebase back), the closed assignment reopens with its history and claim count, so R3.28's attempt cap cannot be reset by an undo; anything else opens a new one.

### The later change and its originating agent (decision 2)

**The later change is the conflicted change itself.** A rebase that conflicts puts the conflict in the commit that was moved: the rebased change, which by construction came later than its destination. jj's rebase of descendants after an amend does the same to the dependents. A merge commit's conflict is the merge's. In every case the conflicted change is the one whose new snapshot resolves it, so its originating agent is the one that has the context to do it.

**The owner** is computed from R3.04's records of that change, re-verified as every provenance read is (R3.25's `provenance_for` and `read_records`):

1. The sessions that recorded the change, newest record first. If any is live (`ended_at` null), the newest live one owns it (reason `DEFAULT`).
2. Else the newest one, ended: the assignment opens with that session as its owner and goes through decision 4's liveness at once (follow, grace, or the queue).
3. A change with no records that verify: the live session of an active Reef workspace whose working-copy commit is that change, if exactly one (reason `DEFAULT`). A workspace created with `--change` on a conflicted change owns it before it records anything.
4. Otherwise the assignment is **`UNOWNED`**: Reef never recorded work on this change, so it is most likely a person's own (a `jj rebase` in their checkout). It is not put in the queue, where a resolver agent would rewrite someone's change uninvited (S-155); a person queues it (`reef conflict assign --queue`), assigns it, or takes it. R3.33 shows it as "a conflict nobody owns".

Sides are not used. A side's agent did not write the conflicted change, and handing it that change would have one agent rewrite another's (or a person's) work; R3.25's `VERIFIED` sides are still what the resolver reads to understand each side. The newest session, not the first ("originating" read literally), because after a takeover the newest has the current context; the first is in the assignment's provenance view for anyone who wants it.

An owner is recorded as its session, its workspace, the identity, actor and agent of that session, and its declared client session, copied into the row so an owner reads the same after `sweep_provenance_sessions` deletes the session.

### "The owner's session has ended" (decision 4)

Observably: **the owner's workspace has no live provenance session**, because it was released (`ws release`, the end of `ws run`) or reclaimed after its lease and grace (35 minutes after an MCP server or a provider's holder stops, by default). A revocation that restarts the session in the same workspace is not an end: the owner moves to the new session silently. Presence is not used: it is ephemeral, and most workspaces have no heartbeat on it (ADR 0037).

Two refinements keep agents that work in short-lived workspaces from losing their conflicts:

- **Follow by client session.** When the owner's session ends, the assignment moves to a live session in the same repository with the same identity and the same non-empty declared `client_session` (R3.44), newest first (reason `FOLLOWED`). An agent that released one workspace and is working in another keeps its conflict, and sees it in that workspace's `reef status`.
- **Grace.** If no such session exists, the assignment stays with the ended owner for 5 minutes (`OWNER_GRACE`, a constant) before it is queued, so an agent between two `ws run` calls is followed into its next workspace rather than queued.
- **Return.** While a queued assignment whose owner ended (reason `OWNER_ENDED`) is unclaimed, a new live session with that owner's identity and client session takes it back (reason `RETURNED`): a resumed harness session gets its conflicts back if nobody else has started on them. An assignment a person queued, or a claimer released, does not return.

`client_session` is declared, not verified, and subagents of one harness session share it, so following can hand a conflict to a sibling of the same session and identity. That is the same agent from the person's point of view, and the identity check keeps it within one identity (S-151).

### States

```text
            follower: owner live              claim (queued only)
  (none) ──────────────────────▶ ASSIGNED ◀──────────────────────────┐
     │                              │  owner ended + grace, no follow │
     │ no owner                     ▼                                 │
     └──────▶ UNOWNED ──assign──▶ QUEUED ─────────────────────────────┘
                 │                  ▲  release by owner, assign --queue
                 └──take──▶ TAKEN ──┘  release by the person
  any open state ── the change no longer carries it ──▶ CLOSED (resolved | abandoned | immutable | inherited)
```

| State | Owner | In the queue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ASSIGNED` | a live session (default, followed, returned, claimed, or assigned to a workspace by a caller) | no |
| `QUEUED` | none current; the last owner is kept for `RETURNED` | yes |
| `TAKEN` | a person identity, no session; held until released or resolved | no |
| `UNOWNED` | none; Reef never recorded the change | no |
| `CLOSED` | as at close | no |

Every change of state or owner has a **reason**: `DEFAULT`, `FOLLOWED`, `RETURNED`, `OWNER_ENDED`, `NO_OWNER`, `CLAIMED`, `RELEASED`, `ASSIGNED` (by a caller, to a workspace or the queue), `TAKEN`, and for closing `RESOLVED`, `ABANDONED`, `IMMUTABLE`, `INHERITED`.

### The queue and its order (decision 5)

The **resolver queue** of a repository is its `QUEUED` assignments: conflicts whose owner's session has ended, that a person queued, or that a claimer released. Live owners' conflicts are not in it: the design gives them to their owner, and a resolver racing a live owner would make two resolutions of one change.

Order: **`priority` descending, then `queued_at` ascending, then id**. `priority` is an integer from -100 to 100, 0 by default, set by `AssignConflict` (a person raising a conflict that blocks a release, or lowering one nobody should touch yet). R3.21's landing-queue priority is a separate field of a separate queue. `queued_at` is when the assignment last entered the queue, so a released claim goes to the back of its priority.

The design's "with a priority" is this integer. Ranking by blocked dependents or by lifecycle state was considered (decision 5's alternatives); both move as the repository changes, so the order would reshuffle under a resolver reading it, and a person can say what matters.

### Claims (decision 6)

A resolver agent **claims** a queued assignment for one of its workspaces: `ClaimConflict { workspace, assignment (an id or a revision; empty for the queue's head), expected_conflict_id }`.

- **A claim is held by the workspace's provenance session**, and lasts as long as it does. When that session ends (the workspace released or reclaimed) and the change still carries the conflict, the assignment goes back through decision 4 (follow, grace, queue) like any owner's. No separate lease, TTL or heartbeat: the workspace lease already is one, with its heartbeat (`ws renew --follow`, the MCP server) and its reclaim, and a resolver needs a workspace to resolve in anyway.
- **What can be claimed**: a `QUEUED` assignment, or one already `ASSIGNED` to the caller's session (no change, no event). `ASSIGNED` to another session or `TAKEN` is refused with `ASSIGNMENT_HELD` (metadata: owner), whose `next` is `reef conflict assign` for a person. `UNOWNED` is refused with `ASSIGNMENT_UNOWNED` (a person decides first). `CLOSED` is `ASSIGNMENT_CLOSED`. An `expected_conflict_id` that differs is `CONFLICT_CHANGED` (`ABORTED`), so a resolver that read the conflict first never claims a different one (S-154).
- **The workspace** must be active in the repository with a live provenance session (`NO_LIVE_SESSION` otherwise). It need not edit the change; the response's hint names `reef ws create --change <change>` when it does not.
- **R3.28's gate.** Claims go through one function, `assign::may_claim`, that allows everything here; R3.28 refuses an agent's claim of an `ask` tier conflict or one over its attempt cap there.
- **`claims`** counts claims on the assignment, for R3.28 and the view.

`ReleaseConflict { assignment, workspace, reason }` gives up an assignment: from its owner's workspace (`ASSIGNED`), or from the person who took it (`TAKEN`), to `QUEUED` with reason `RELEASED`. Anyone else gets `ASSIGNMENT_HELD`. The originating agent releasing its default assignment ("I cannot resolve this") is the same call.

The CLI's **`reef conflict claim --next`** is the resolver's one command: outside a workspace it reads the queue's head, creates a workspace editing that change (`ws create --change`, purpose `resolve <change>`), and claims it there with the conflict id it read; on `ASSIGNMENT_HELD` or `CONFLICT_CHANGED` (another resolver won) it releases that workspace and tries the next, at most three times. It prints the workspace and the assignment, and `next: reef conflict show -r <change> --hunks`.

### Reassignment and takeover (decision 7)

`AssignConflict { assignment, expected_conflict_id, target, priority, reason }`, where `target` is one of:

- **`workspace`**: assign to that active workspace's live session (reason `ASSIGNED`). A person directing a particular agent, or an orchestrator handing out work.
- **`queue`**: put it in the queue (reason `ASSIGNED`), from any open state, `UNOWNED` included.
- **`take`**: **manual takeover by a person**. The assignment becomes `TAKEN` with the caller's identity as owner and no session; it does not end with any workspace and stays until the person releases it or the change no longer carries the conflict. The person resolves it however they like (a workspace, or the jj CLI in their checkout). Refused over the remote listener (`REMOTE_HUMAN`, as R3.09's decline is) and when the call's actor is `agent` (`TAKE_NEEDS_PERSON`, `next: reef conflict claim`), since an agent claims.

With no `target`, `priority` alone changes the priority. Any WRITE caller may assign: one uid is one trust domain (ADR 0027), and every assignment records who made it (actor, agent, identity, session, remote token), as R3.09's transitions do (S-152). Assigning over a live claim or a takeover is allowed and is the override; the previous owner learns from the operation (decision 10) and its next `reef status`.

### When the conflict disappears (decision 8)

The follower closes an open assignment when its change no longer carries the conflict at the head:

| Close reason | When |
| --- | --- |
| `RESOLVED` | The change has a visible, mutable commit with no introduced conflict of this kind. `closed_commit_id` is that commit; `resolved_session_id` is the session whose provenance record names that commit, when the daemon recorded it (a workspace snapshot or `EditFiles`), and empty when it came from elsewhere (the jj CLI, a rebase that made the conflict go away). |
| `INHERITED` | The change's commit is still conflicted, but every conflicted file is now inherited from a parent, whose own assignment covers it. |
| `ABANDONED` | The change has no visible commit: abandoned, squashed away, or hidden by an undo. |
| `IMMUTABLE` | The change's commit left `mutable()` (it reached `trunk()` by a means that does not refuse conflicts, or the immutable set was configured wider). |

"Resolved" means the change no longer has the conflict, not that someone fixed it by hand; `resolved_session_id` and the closing commit's provenance say who wrote the commit that has none. R3.28 marks resolutions in the proposal flow from that.

A conflict that **changes but stays** (a new conflict id: a partial resolution, a re-rebase) keeps its assignment, owner and claims; the row's `conflict_id` and `conflict_changed_at` move, and an event says so (reason `CHANGED`, owner unchanged). Reappearance within 24 hours with the same id reopens (decision 1).

### Storage: schema 17 (decision 9)

`0017_conflict_assignments.sql` (the number the rebases gave it; R3.14 took 15 and R3.12 16):

```sql
-- R3.27: resolver assignments (ADR 0068). One open row per (repository, change, kind) for a change
-- that introduces a conflict; the follower opens, updates and closes rows as the operation log
-- moves. Owners are copied in so a row reads the same after its session is swept. Times are
-- milliseconds since the Unix epoch. Released migrations are never edited.
CREATE TABLE conflict_assignments(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, id TEXT NOT NULL,       -- asg_<16 hex>
             change_id TEXT NOT NULL, kind TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- tree
             commit_id TEXT NOT NULL, conflict_id TEXT NOT NULL, first_conflict_id TEXT NOT NULL,
             introduced INTEGER NOT NULL, files_total INTEGER NOT NULL,
             state TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- assigned queued taken unowned closed
             reason TEXT NOT NULL,                    -- why the state and owner are what they are
             owner_session_id TEXT, owner_workspace_id TEXT, owner_identity_id TEXT,
             owner_actor TEXT, owner_agent TEXT, owner_client_session TEXT,
             owner_ended_at INTEGER,                  -- the owner's session ended; grace runs from here
             priority INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, queued_at INTEGER,
             claims INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, events INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
             opened_at INTEGER NOT NULL, updated_at INTEGER NOT NULL, conflict_changed_at INTEGER NOT NULL,
             closed_at INTEGER, close_reason TEXT, closed_commit_id TEXT, resolved_session_id TEXT,
             PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, id)) WITHOUT ROWID;
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX conflict_assignments_open ON conflict_assignments(repo_id, change_id, kind)
             WHERE closed_at IS NULL;
CREATE INDEX conflict_assignments_queue ON conflict_assignments(repo_id, priority DESC, queued_at, id)
             WHERE state = 'queued';
CREATE INDEX conflict_assignments_by_owner ON conflict_assignments(owner_workspace_id) WHERE closed_at IS NULL;
CREATE INDEX conflict_assignments_by_client ON conflict_assignments(owner_client_session) WHERE closed_at IS NULL;
CREATE INDEX conflict_assignments_closed ON conflict_assignments(repo_id, closed_at) WHERE closed_at IS NOT NULL;
CREATE TABLE conflict_assignment_events(repo_id TEXT NOT NULL, id TEXT NOT NULL, -- cae_<16 hex>
             seq INTEGER NOT NULL,                    -- per repository, in commit order
             assignment_id TEXT NOT NULL, change_id TEXT NOT NULL, conflict_id TEXT NOT NULL,
             from_state TEXT, to_state TEXT NOT NULL, reason TEXT NOT NULL,
             owner_session_id TEXT, owner_workspace_id TEXT, owner_identity_id TEXT, owner_agent TEXT,
             priority INTEGER NOT NULL, note TEXT,    -- the caller's reason, at most 4 KiB
             by_actor TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- human agent daemon
             by_agent TEXT, by_identity_id TEXT, by_session_id TEXT, by_remote_token TEXT,
             at INTEGER NOT NULL,
             PRIMARY KEY (repo_id, id)) WITHOUT ROWID;
CREATE INDEX conflict_assignment_events_by_assignment ON conflict_assignment_events(repo_id, assignment_id, seq);
CREATE INDEX conflict_assignment_events_by_seq ON conflict_assignment_events(repo_id, seq);
```

- **Rows hold no operation ids**, so GC's rewrite of operation ids (R2.16) needs no mapping here; the operation names the events instead (decision 10).
- **Bounds.** At most 1,000 open assignments per repository: past it, the follower logs once per look and leaves the rest unassigned (`reef conflict list` still shows them). At most 1,000 events per assignment: a claim, release or assign past it is refused with `ASSIGNMENT_EVENT_LIMIT` (`RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`, `next: reef conflict take`), so a claim loop cannot grow the table without bound before R3.28's cap exists (S-153).
- **Retention.** GC's tenth step deletes closed assignments, with their events, 30 days after they closed (a constant, in `docs/conflicts.md`). `repo forget` deletes every row of the repository. Backups copy them with the database, as everything in it.

### The follower (decision 9)

One task, as `lifecycle::observe` is: woken by every operation (`state.events.changes()`), by a provenance session starting or ending (a `Notify` that `ProvenanceSessions` pokes), and by its own timer at the earliest grace deadline, settling 200 ms and looking at a repository at most once a second. Per repository whose head operation moved, or with a deadline due, under the repository's **assignment lock** (a `tokio::sync::Mutex` per repository, like the lifecycle lock):

1. Evaluate `mutable() & conflicts()` at the head (at most 10,000 commits). For each conflicted change whose newest commit differs from its open row's `commit_id`, `conflict::read` with no files (id, `introduced`, `files_total`); a commit already looked at is not read again.
2. Open (or reopen) assignments for changes that introduce a conflict and have none, with their owner (decision 2); update `conflict_id` where it changed; close the open rows whose change is no longer in the set, after one lookup of the change's visible commits for the reason (decision 8).
3. Check the liveness of every `ASSIGNED` row's owner workspace (one query over the open rows' workspaces): ended owners follow, wait out their grace, or go to the queue; queued rows whose ended owner's client session is live again return (decision 4).
4. Write the rows and their events in one SQLite transaction, then record one operation carrying every event of the pass (decision 10).

**Reads and writes look first.** Every assignment RPC runs step 1 to 3 for its repository before it answers, when the head moved since the last look, so a claim never acts on a conflict the follower has not seen, and a read right after a rebase sees it. `GetStatus` does not: it is the hot path (R2.23), and reads the rows as they are, at most a second behind.

At start the follower looks at every repository once, so assignments catch up with whatever happened while the daemon was down (a workspace reclaimed at start ends its session through the same path).

### Live updates: an operation per change (decision 10)

Every change of an assignment is an event row and, after the rows commit, part of a jj **operation that changes nothing in the view**, with a `reef.conflict` attribute carrying the pass's events (`{event id, seq, assignment, change, kind, conflict_id, from, to, reason, owner workspace, owner agent}`, at most 64 events per operation, several operations for a larger pass). Claims, releases and assigns record one operation each, with the caller's agent and workspace in the usual `reef.*` attributes; the follower's operations are the daemon's.

- **Why the operation log**: it is how every client already learns what changed. `SubscribeOperations` delivers it within 100 ms with its filters; the web UI's activity feed and R3.33's inbox read it; MCP's `wait_for_operations` waits on it; `reef op log` shows it. R3.09's transitions and R3.13's landings are carried the same way.
- **A new kind**, `OPERATION_KIND_CONFLICT_ASSIGNMENT` (the next free number), and `Operation.conflict_events` (repeated `ConflictAssignmentEvent`). `OpMatcher` matches an event by its change (the `changes` filter) and by its owner's workspace (the `workspaces` filter), so an agent watching its workspace sees the assignment arrive.
- **Rows first, then the operation.** The rows are the truth; the operation is the notice. A crash between the two leaves events no operation carries: a subscriber misses that notice, and the next list shows the state. This is lighter than R3.09's pending-row protocol because nothing reads the operation back to decide state (S-158).
- **Undo** refuses an operation that carries `reef.conflict` ("it records conflict assignments; release or assign the conflict instead"), as it refuses `reef.lifecycle`; reverting an operation that changed nothing in the view would change nothing anyway.

### The API: `ConflictService`, the next API revision (decision 11)

Five RPCs on R3.25's service, in `conflicts.proto`. Every field and enum value gets a comment.

```proto

enum ConflictAssignmentState { …_UNSPECIFIED = 0; ASSIGNED = 1; QUEUED = 2; TAKEN = 3; UNOWNED = 4; CLOSED = 5; }

  string workspace = 3; int32 page_size = 4; string page_token = 5; }           // events, a page at a time
```

- **`ListConflictAssignments`** returns `QUEUED` rows in queue order (with `queue_position`), then the rest by `updated_at`, newest first; `page_size` 0 for 50, at most 200. With `workspace`, it returns what that workspace's session owns, which is what `--mine` asks. `CLOSED` only when asked.
- **`GetConflictAssignment`** returns one assignment and its events, oldest first, 100 a page by default, at most 1,000.
- **On R3.25's messages**: `Conflict.assignment` (the change's open assignment of that kind, as it is now, even for a read at an older operation; unset when there is none), and `WorkspaceStatus.conflict_assignments` (the open assignments the workspace's session owns, at most 10, without events) with `conflict_assignment_count`.

- **Remote listener**: the two reads for any READ token; `ClaimConflict`, `ReleaseConflict` and `AssignConflict` get rows in `remote/rules.rs` (`write` scope; `take` refused by the handler, as above).
- **Review checklist**: ADR 0029's, item by item in the PR. `API_REVISION` the next free number, with its digest. `ListConflictAssignments` has one repeated field; `GetConflictAssignment` is paged by `events` (one repeated field at the top), so `sdk/fixtures/page-items.json` names `events`.

### The CLI (decision 12)

```
reef conflict queue                                  # the queue, in order
reef conflict assignments [--mine] [--state S]… [-r REV] [--closed]
reef conflict assignment ID|REV                      # one, with its events
reef conflict claim [ID|REV | --next] [--workspace WS] [--expect CONFLICT_ID]
reef conflict release [ID|REV] [--reason TEXT]
reef conflict assign ID|REV (--workspace WS | --queue) [--priority N] [--reason TEXT]
reef conflict assign ID|REV --priority N
reef conflict take ID|REV [--reason TEXT]
```

- **`--mine`** and the default `--workspace` are the workspace containing the current directory, as `reef status` finds it. `release` with no id releases the one assignment the workspace owns, and refuses with the list when it owns several.
- **`claim --next`** is decision 6's one command. Inside a workspace, `claim` binds to it and creates nothing.
- **`reef conflict list`** gains an owner column (`queued`, `taken by arsood`, `agent claude-x ws 7d73…`, `unowned`), from `Conflict.assignment`.
- **`reef status`** prints, under a workspace's conflicted paths, `Assigned to you: <n> conflict(s): reef conflict assignments --mine`; `-o json` carries `conflict_assignments`. Its brief view gains the count.
- **Exit codes** as ADR 0025 maps the kinds; every refusal carries `hint` and `next`.
- **JSON** types in `reef-vcs`'s `cli/conflict.rs`, as R3.25's are, reusing `json::Change`; `reef schema`'s `OUTPUTS` gains a row for each new command; brief views carry `id`, `change_id`, `state`, `owner.agent`, `priority`, `queue_position`.
- Text output escapes control characters in reasons, agents and descriptions (S-130's rule).

- **`docs/adr/0068-resolver-assignment.md`** (accepted), indexed in `docs/adr/README.md`.
  - **Decisions**:
    1. an assignment is keyed by change and kind, one open at a time, for conflicts a change introduces; the conflict id is tracked, not the key; the same conflict back within 24 hours reopens;
    2. the later change is the conflicted change; its owner is the newest live session that recorded it, else its newest session, else a live workspace editing it, else none (`UNOWNED`, never queued automatically); sides are not owners;
    3. an owner's session has ended when its workspace has no live provenance session; assignments follow the same identity and declared client session, wait 5 minutes, and return to a resumed owner while unclaimed;
    4. the queue is the `QUEUED` assignments, ordered by a person-set priority then time queued;
    5. a claim is held by the claiming workspace's provenance session, with no lease of its own; only queued assignments are claimed; R3.28 gates claims through one function;
    6. any WRITE caller reassigns or queues, recorded with who; takeover is a person's, on the socket;
    7. the follower closes an assignment when its change no longer carries the conflict, with a reason, and records who wrote the closing commit when the daemon did;
    8. rows are the truth and an operation per change of state is the notice, refused by undo.
  - **Alternatives**: keying by conflict id or file id (the resolver's own progress would close and reopen it); deriving assignments on every read with no table (claims, takeovers, queue times and history are facts only Reef knows); owners from the sides (another agent's change rewritten); the first session as owner (stale after a takeover); presence or lease heartbeats as liveness (not persisted; most workspaces have none); a claim lease with its own heartbeat (a second lease beside the workspace's); queueing every conflict with live owners ranked last (two resolutions of one change); ranking by dependents or lifecycle (reshuffles under the reader); R3.09's pending-row protocol for the operation (nothing reads the operation back for state); a stream of its own (the operation log already reaches every client); an MCP tool (budget; `get_status` tells the agent).

2. `owner_ended_queues_after_grace`: releasing the owner's workspace leaves the assignment `ASSIGNED` until the grace passes, then `QUEUED` with `OWNER_ENDED` and `queued_at`; a reclaim does the same.
3. `fallbacks` (S-155): a conflicted change with no records but a workspace editing it is assigned to that workspace; one with neither is `UNOWNED`, not queued, and refused by `claim` with `ASSIGNMENT_UNOWNED`; `assign --queue` queues it.

5. `follow_and_return` (S-151): with a declared client session, releasing the owner's workspace while another workspace of the same identity and client session is live moves the assignment there (`FOLLOWED`); a different identity with the same client session does not; a queued `OWNER_ENDED` assignment returns (`RETURNED`) when a new workspace of the owner's session appears, and does not once claimed or once a person queued it.
6. `close_reasons`: editing the conflicted file in the owner's workspace and snapshotting closes it `RESOLVED` with `resolved_session_id`; a jj-lib rewrite that resolves it closes it with that empty; abandoning the change is `ABANDONED`; resolving the parent while the child had its own copy closes the child's as `INHERITED`; a partial resolution keeps it open with a new `conflict_id` and a `CHANGED` event; undoing the resolution within 24 hours reopens the same assignment with its claims.
7. `claims` (S-154): `claim` from a second workspace of a queued assignment makes it `ASSIGNED`/`CLAIMED`, `claims` 1; a second claimer gets `ASSIGNMENT_HELD` with `next`; a wrong `expected_conflict_id` is `CONFLICT_CHANGED`; an empty `assignment` claims the queue's head, and an empty queue is `QUEUE_EMPTY`; releasing the claimer's workspace returns it to the queue; `release` by the claimer queues it with `RELEASED` at the back of its priority.
8. `assign_and_take` (S-152, S-156): `assign --workspace` over a live claim moves it and records the caller; `--priority` reorders the queue; `take` on the socket makes it `TAKEN` by the default person identity, which no session end affects; `take` over the remote listener is `REMOTE_HUMAN`, and from an agent actor `TAKE_NEEDS_PERSON`; a `write` token claims, releases and assigns; a `read` token is refused all three and lists.
9. `bounds` (S-153): the 1,001st open assignment is not opened and is logged; an assignment at 1,000 events refuses a claim with `ASSIGNMENT_EVENT_LIMIT`; a pass with 100 events writes two operations.
10. `gc`: a closed assignment older than 30 days is deleted with its events by GC; an open one is kept; `repo forget` deletes all.
11. `restart` (S-158): a daemon restarted after a workspace was released while it was down queues the assignment on its first look; an event whose operation was never written still lists.
12. `undo`: `UndoOperation` of an assignment operation is refused with its reason.

## Implementation notes

- **Order**: the migration and `db::conflict_assignments`; owners and liveness as pure functions over rows, unit-tested; the follower (tests 1 to 6, 11); claims and assigns (7, 8); the operation, filter and undo (1, 12); bounds and GC (9, 10); the proto and service; the CLI (13); the documents.
- **Building.** Every local build sets `CARGO_INCREMENTAL=0` and `CARGO_PROFILE_DEV_DEBUG=line-tables-only` with a target directory of its own, and checks for 50 GiB free first. No `cfg(target_os)` code, so the hosted Linux leg covers Linux.
- **Locks.** The assignment lock is taken without the create lock: nothing here rewrites a commit or moves a workspace. `claim --next`'s workspace creation is the CLI's, outside any lock. The follower never holds the assignment lock across a jj read longer than one pass of one repository.
- **The follower reads through the reading layer** at the head, on the repository thread, with `skip_snapshot` (it must not snapshot workspaces to look for conflicts; the watcher's snapshots already wake it).
- **The provenance join** reuses R3.25's `provenance_for` and the session reader, so owners come only from records that verify.
- **No new configuration.** `OWNER_GRACE` (5 minutes), the reopen window (24 hours), retention (30 days) and the bounds are constants, in `docs/conflicts.md`; the grace has a test hook.

## Out of scope

- **Tiers and attempt caps**: R3.28, through `may_claim` and the `claims` count; it decides what an attempt is (a claim that ended without a resolution is one reading).
- **Resolutions in the proposal flow** ("marked as a resolution"): R3.28, from `resolved_session_id` and the closing commit.
- **Intent escalation**: R3.29, which may add a release reason that asks a person.
- **Backpressure**: R3.30, which counts unresolved conflict ancestors from jj, not from these rows.
- **The conflict view and the inbox**: R3.35 (resolver status, takeover button) and R3.33 (unowned and queued conflicts).
- **Conflicts jj does not store**: R3.26 and R3.11 open assignments of their kinds through the same table and follower seam.
- **Writing conflicts**: the landing service refuses a rebase that conflicts (R3.13), and returning a proposal "to its owner with a conflict object" needs a landing that writes the conflicted rebase, which is R3.14's or R3.11's choice. Whatever writes one, this follower assigns it.
- **Pushing to an agent**: resuming an ended harness session to resolve its conflict is R3.12's mechanism (comments as instructions resume "the originating agent's session"); this ticket queues instead.
- **An MCP tool**: decision 13.

## Risks

- **Follower cost under a busy fleet.** A revset over `mutable()` per look, at most once a second per repository. Mitigations: commits already read are not read again; the load test is run with it; the look interval is a constant to raise if the numbers say so.
- **Liveness lags the agent.** A crashed agent's workspace lives until its lease and grace run out (S-157), and an agent between `ws run` calls without a declared client session is queued after the grace. Mitigations: the grace, following, return, and a person's `assign --queue`; documented.
- **Shared client sessions.** Subagents of one harness session share the declared session, so a conflict follows to a sibling. Documented; it stays within one identity.
- **Two writers of one change.** A resolver claims while the original owner, still alive in a workspace that edits the change, also edits it: impossible by construction (a live owner's conflict is never queued), except after a person's reassignment, which is that person's call. jj makes the change divergent if both rewrite it, which `reef status` reports.

## Choices this brief made, and why

- **Key by change**, because the conflict id moves with the resolver's own progress.
- **The conflicted change is the later change**, because it is the one moved onto the other, and the one whose snapshot resolves it.
- **Session end is the provenance session's end**, because it is persisted and observable, unlike presence.
- **Follow, grace and return**, because agents work in short-lived workspaces and resume sessions.
- **Claims ride on the workspace's session**, because a resolver needs a workspace and the workspace already has a lease.
- **Unowned changes wait for a person**, because a change Reef never recorded is probably a person's.
- **An operation per change**, because the operation log is how every client learns of change.

## What later tickets inherit

- **R3.28** gates claims in `may_claim` (tiers, caps), counts attempts from `claims` and the events, and marks resolutions from `resolved_session_id`; it extends `--policy-input`.
- **R3.29** adds an escalation release reason.
- **R3.26** and **R3.11** open assignments of their kinds through the follower's seam.
- **R3.33** lists `UNOWNED` and long-`QUEUED` assignments as needs-you-now, live from the operation kind.
- **R3.35** shows owner, state, claims and events, and calls `AssignConflict` for takeover.
- **R3.12** may resume an ended owner's session for its conflict instead of queueing it.
- **RX.02** reviews S-151 to S-158.

## Departures from dev-phases.md

- **"The originating agent of the later change"** is the newest live session that recorded the conflicted change (the later change is the conflicted one), from R3.04's records, followed to the same identity's other live session by its declared client session; a change Reef never recorded is `UNOWNED` and waits for a person.
- **"Whose owner's session has ended"** is its workspace having no live provenance session (released or reclaimed), after a 5-minute grace; a resumed owner gets an unclaimed conflict back.
- **"Resolver queue with priority"** is the queued assignments of a repository, ordered by a person-set priority, then the time they were queued; resolvers claim from it, a claim lasting as long as the claiming workspace's session.
- **The ticket adds** reassignment and a person's takeover (the design's "a human can still take a conflict over"), state in `reef.sqlite3` (schema 17, after R3.14's and R3.12's), an operation per change of state, five RPCs, `reef conflict` subcommands and `reef status`'s line, which the row did not name.

## Departures in the implementation

Found while building it (2026-11-09):

1. **The shared types are in a new file, `assignments.proto`** (the states, the reasons, `ConflictOwner` and `ConflictAssignmentEvent`), imported by `operations.proto` and `conflicts.proto`: `Operation.conflict_events` cannot name a type of `conflicts.proto`, which imports `operations.proto` through `changes.proto`. `ConflictAssignment` itself, with its `ConflictKind`, stays in `conflicts.proto`.
2. **The writes return `Operation operation`**, not an operation id: ADR 0029's rule that every WRITE response carries its operation (`reef-api`'s `writes_return_operations` test). A claim of an assignment the caller already holds returns none.
3. **Error codes.** Reef's error kinds have no `ABORTED` or `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`, so `CONFLICT_CHANGED` and `ASSIGNMENT_EVENT_LIMIT` are `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (exit 5), like the other refusals. A remote `take` reuses R3.09's `REMOTE_HUMAN_REQUIRED`. `AssignConflict` with neither a target nor a priority is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`/`ASSIGNMENT_TARGET_REQUIRED`.
4. **A remote claim or release names a workspace the caller's token created** (`Rule::OwnWorkspace` in `remote/rules.rs`), as releasing a workspace does; `AssignConflict` is `Rule::None`, with `take` refused by the handler.
5. **A reason the brief did not list, `reopened`**, marks an assignment reopened within 24 hours, so its events say why it came back.
6. **No session notifications.** Releases, reclaims and creates each record an operation, which wakes the follower; the follower also wakes at the earliest grace deadline and at least once a minute. Its one-second interval is between passes over every repository, not per repository.
7. **The CLI's `assign` target is `--to WS`**, since `--workspace` is every command's scope flag (the caller's workspace). JSON spells reasons in kebab case (`owner-ended`, `no-owner`), as R3.25's file kinds are; the daemon's columns use snake case.
8. **`sdk/fixtures/page-items.json` is unchanged**: `GetConflictAssignmentResponse` and `ListConflictAssignmentsResponse` each have one repeated field at the top, which the SDK generators take as the items without an entry.
9. **GC's tenth step reports no count** in `GcReport` (no API field for it); it logs what it deleted, skips a dry run, and uses the earlier of 30 days ago and GC's `keep_newer`.
10. **`reef status`'s line** reads `Assigned to you: N conflict(s) to resolve: reef conflict assignments --mine`, and `-o json` carries `conflict_assignments` and `conflict_assignment_count` only when there are some; the brief view carries each one's id, change and state. `reef conflict list` gains a `RESOLVER` column and `show` a `resolver` line. The agent guide is unchanged: its long part is at its 1,600-byte budget.
11. **`reef_client::json::Operation` does not gain the events**: they are in its `attributes` (`reef.conflict`), and only the proto's `Operation.conflict_events` parses them.
12. **Tests the brief listed that are not written**: the 1,001st open assignment and a 100-event pass (test 9; the event cap is a unit test), `inherited` and `immutable` closes (test 6), and the restart case (test 11). Test numbering in `conflict_assignments.rs` follows the brief's otherwise.
13. **Renumbered at the rebases onto R3.14 and R3.12** (2026-11-09): schema 17 (`0017_conflict_assignments`), API revision 28, `OPERATION_KIND_CONFLICT_ASSIGNMENT = 16`, `Operation.conflict_events = 22`.
14. **The review changed these** (the PR's table has every finding):
    - **The follower's `changed` events** are at most one a minute per assignment (the rest update the row quietly), since each snapshot of a partial resolution changes the conflict id; S-153 now says the follower's events are bounded by how often operations happen, not capped.
    - **An unowned assignment stays unowned** until a caller moves it (the follower no longer promotes it), and one whose only owner was a workspace editing an unrecorded change goes back to `unowned`, not to the queue, when that workspace ends (S-155).
    - **An agent assigning to a workspace is held to a claim's rules**: a queued assignment only, through `may_claim` (S-152).
    - **A takeover is in the daemon's default person identity** (a remote token's linked one), whatever workspace the call names, so `release` agrees with it; a takeover is the one change allowed past the 1,000-event cap.
    - **A notice carries the previous owner's workspace** (`from_workspace`; `ConflictAssignmentEvent.from_workspace_id`), and the workspace filter matches it, so an overridden owner sees the operation.
    - **The scan's cap counts conflicts a change introduces** (1,000, and 8,000 conflicted changes in all), not the brief's 10,000 commits, so a tall stack of inheriting children cannot hide the commit that introduces a conflict.
    - **`resolved_session_id`** comes only from a record that verifies and was made after the assignment opened.
    - **The event seq** is kept in `meta` (`assignment_seq:<repo>`), so retention never lets it go back; a repository forgotten during a look gets no rows.
    - **The follower's own operations do not make the next look read jj again**; a look still reads every conflicted commit whenever another operation moved the head (the brief's "a commit already looked at is not read again" is not done), and the reads that look first are not rate-limited.
    - **The follower's database and provenance work runs on the blocking pool.**
    - **`release` with no id**, from a workspace that owns none or several, is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`/`ASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` with the ids it owns; `claim --next` also retries when the picked conflict was closed or its change went away.
    - **Kept as they are**: the assignment lock is held while the daemon records its operation, and READ RPCs that look first may record the follower's rows and operations (a remote `read` token included); both are documented here and in the PR.
15. **The follower looks at the head the daemon has loaded** (`look_loaded`), not a reloaded one: reloading merged concurrent operation heads that nothing had asked to merge (CI's `subscribe::outside_writes_arrive`). Outside writes are looked at once the daemon next loads them, as any read or snapshot does; the RPCs' looks still reload.
16. **No benchmark row.** `docs/benchmarks.md` has no "Conflict assignments (R3.27)" section: the daemon tests run each pass in milliseconds; a measurement is a follow-up with R3.35's load.

## Decisions for the user

Grouped for asking; the brief's body follows every recommendation. Answered 2026-11-09: every recommendation accepted (1 to 13), including decision 4's follow, grace and return; following a client session that subagents share stays documented known behaviour (S-151).

**Group 1: what is assigned, to whom**

1. **Key.** One open assignment per (change, kind), for conflicts the change introduces; the conflict id is tracked and checked, not the key; the same conflict back within 24 hours reopens. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: key by conflict id (closed and reopened by every partial resolution); key by file id (one assignment per path, many per change).
2. **Later change and originating agent.** The conflicted change is the later change; its owner is the newest live session that recorded it, else its newest (ended) session, else a live workspace editing it, else `UNOWNED`, which is never queued without a person. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: the first session ("originating" literally); fall back to the later `VERIFIED` side's agent when the change has no records; queue unowned conflicts like the rest.
3. **Which conflicts.** Introduced only, visible and mutable, tree conflicts now, with `kind` for R3.26 and R3.11. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: every conflicted commit, inherited ones included.

**Group 2: liveness and the queue**

4. **"Session has ended".** The owner's workspace has no live provenance session (release, reclaim), not presence; follow to a live session of the same identity and declared client session; 5-minute grace; return to a resumed owner while unclaimed. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: no follow or return (simpler; ws-run agents lose conflicts at once); a longer grace (10 to 30 minutes); presence heartbeats.
5. **Queue membership and order.** Only conflicts with no live owner (ended, released, or queued by a person); order by priority (-100 to 100, set by a person) then time queued. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: every open conflict in the queue, ended owners first; rank by blocked dependents or lifecycle state.
6. **Claims.** A claim is held by the claiming workspace's provenance session (no separate lease); only queued conflicts are claimed; `claim --next` creates a workspace editing the change; R3.28 gates claims in one function. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: a claim lease with its own TTL and heartbeat; a claim with no workspace.
7. **Reassignment and takeover.** Any WRITE caller assigns to a workspace, queues, or sets priority, recorded with who; `take` is a person's, on the socket only, and survives any session; an override of a live claim is allowed. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: only persons reassign; `take` also over the remote listener for tokens linked to a person identity.
8. **Disappearance.** Close as `RESOLVED`, `INHERITED`, `ABANDONED` or `IMMUTABLE`, with the closing commit and the session that recorded it; a changed conflict keeps its assignment. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: close on any conflict id change and open a new one.

**Group 3: storage and surface**

9. **Storage.** Schema 15: `conflict_assignments` and `conflict_assignment_events`; a follower task on the operation log, looking at most once a second; reads and writes look first; 1,000 open per repository, 1,000 events per assignment, closed rows kept 30 days. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: no table, assignments derived on read with only claims and takeovers stored (loses queue time, history and live events).
10. **Live updates.** One operation per change of state, of a new kind, carrying the events, filterable by change and owner workspace, refused by undo; rows first, so a crash can lose a notice but never state. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternatives: rows only, clients poll; R3.09's pending-row protocol for exact operations (heavier); a stream of its own.
11. **API.** Five RPCs on `ConflictService` (`ListConflictAssignments`, `GetConflictAssignment` READ; `ClaimConflict`, `ReleaseConflict`, `AssignConflict` WRITE), `Conflict.assignment`, `WorkspaceStatus.conflict_assignments`; the next API revision. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: a separate `ResolverService`.
12. **CLI.** `reef conflict queue|assignments|assignment|claim|release|assign|take`, an owner column in `reef conflict list`, and a line in `reef status`; all under `--help-all`. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: fold `take` into `assign --take`, and `queue` into `assignments --state queued`.
13. **MCP.** The five RPCs in `NOT_TOOLS`; agents learn of assignments through `get_status`'s new field and `wait_for_operations`, and claim with the CLI. *Recommended*: as stated. Alternative: a `claim_conflict` tool (about 700 bytes), trimming other descriptions to fit the 21,250-byte budget.
