# R3.45 Automatic landing and evidence requests

Phase 3 · Size L (decisions 5 and 6 folded in) · Depends on: R3.13 (the gate, `LandChange`, landing rows, the queue and worker), R3.05 (`Executor::submit`, the job table, dedupe by spec, `cancel`), and builds on R3.07 (`[[require]]` results with `missing` and `failed` needs, the policy read from the destination), R3.09 (lifecycle rows and transitions), R3.10 (stacks: the gate's prefix, sign-offs counting downward), R3.12 (instructions, routed to the proposing session), R3.14 (`[landing]`, the batch's job requests) and R3.17 (a landing's push) · Unblocks: R3.18 (a minimal check set replaces the requested pairs), R3.20 (budgets cap the requested jobs), R3.21 (priority orders proposals and their jobs), R3.31 and R3.33 (the proposal view and inbox show the automatic status); closes #328

## Context

The design's Landing section: "An agent proposes a change plus evidence, or a stack of dependent changes as one proposal. The landing service verifies evidence against policy ... and lands." Its CI load section: "The landing queue tests a batch head rather than each proposal." Neither says a person must ask. Today two things still need someone to type a command:

1. **Landing.** R3.13 lands on request only (`LandChange`, `reef land`). It left the rest to this row: "Landing what policy allows without anyone asking needs a second trigger system (evidence, provenance and policy changes) and per-proposal evaluation keys".
2. **Evidence on the submitted tree.** The gate requires every `[[require]]` pair on the change's own tree, also under `[landing] checks = "batch"` (the gate builds `JudgeContext` with no `evidence_tree`, `landing/mod.rs:1388`, and `judge_with` falls back to the change's tree, `:719`). Only a batch asks an executor for anything (`batch.rs:1323`), and only for its tested tree. So an agent must run `reef job submit --kind test` on its own change before `reef land` can pass, which is what the starter policy's comment tells it to do (`reef-policy/src/lib.rs`, `STARTER`).

Every earlier ticket left a seam here:

- **R3.05**: "Nobody requests checks before R3.13 and R3.14"; `SubmitJob` is not an MCP tool because "an agent proposes, it does not schedule" (`reef-vcs/src/mcp/tools.rs:820`).
- **R3.13**: "R3.45 reuses the gate as is, adds triggers and per-proposal evaluation keys, and submits jobs for `missing` pairs through `Executor::submit` by revision."
- **R3.14**: `[landing]` "R3.45 adds `automatic` to the same table"; "R3.45 requests evidence for proposals' submitted trees with `request_pairs`" (the function is `request`, `batch.rs:1323`).
- **R3.07**: `docs/policy.md` says "R3.45 requests the missing evidence from the named attestor sets".
- **R3.10**: a stack lands by prefix, and a sign-off on a member counts for the members below it. EvaluatePolicy over a stack is follow-up #328.

The direction since 2026-11-09: agents are the users, and a standard MacBook must run five tickets at once. An agent that proposes should see its work checked and landed, or told why not, without a person in the loop; and none of that may cost CPU or disk while nothing changes.

So this ticket builds:

1. **The automatic service**: one task per repository that keeps every open proposal's **automatic status**, re-evaluating a proposal only when its **evaluation key** changes or an event the key cannot see marks it.
2. **Evidence requests**: executor jobs, by revision, for the `[[require]]` pairs a proposal's evaluation reports missing on its own tree, deduped, retried once, capped, and stopped while no executor answers.
3. **Automatic landing**: when the destination's policy says `[landing] automatic = true`, a proposal (or a stack's passing prefix) the gate allows is queued with no request.
4. **Telling the proposing agent**: when automatic landing gives up on a change for a reason its author can fix, the daemon writes one R3.12 instruction to the session that proposed it (decision 5).
5. **`EvaluatePolicy` over a stack** (#328): the gate's stack judgment as a read, so an agent can ask what landing its stack would do (decision 6).
6. **The surface**: the status on every proposal (`ProposalEntry.automatic`, `reef proposal show|list`), `reef land` waiting for requested evidence, `reef policy eval --stack`, and the documents.

### The automatic status (the data shape)

Each open proposal, `PROPOSED` for a destination, has one **automatic status**: what the daemon is doing about it and what it waits for. It lives in memory, in the automatic service, keyed by `(repo, change)`, and is rebuilt after a restart by evaluating every open proposal once.

| State | Means | Next for the agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `off` | The destination's policy neither requests evidence nor lands automatically (`checks = "proposal"` and no `automatic`), or there is no valid policy. | `reef land <id>` |
| `pending` | Not evaluated since the daemon started or since it changed. | wait |
| `waiting` | Policy reports `[[require]]` pairs missing on its tree, and jobs for them are queued or running. Lists the pairs and job ids. | `reef land <id>` waits for them |
| `ready` | The gate would allow it now, and the destination does not land automatically. | `reef land <id>` |
| `queued` | The service queued it (`automatic = true`); names the landing. | `reef landing show <id>` |
| `needs` | Blocked by something no job provides: a sign-off, a failed check on its tree, a free rule, a conflict with the destination, an empty change, an unproposed or amended member of its stack, an unset identity (R3.17). The message is the gate's; `missing` lists sign-offs needed. | what the gate's reason says |
| `no_executor` | Jobs it needs expired unclaimed, and no executor has claimed a job of the repository since. | `reef executor run` |
| `returned` | The service queued it, and the landing returned it (`checks`, `conflicted`, `policy`, and the rest) with nothing changed since; it is not queued again until its key changes. | the landing's reason |

The status is a cache: the gate decides every landing, so a stale status can delay a landing but never let one through (S-223).

### The evaluation key

A proposal is evaluated again only when its **key** changes. The key is a SHA-256 over what the gate reads that can be read cheaply, with no signature checked and no diff:

- the proposal's recorded commit, and the recorded commits of the stack members it is judged with (the stack's shape);
- the blob id of `reef-policy.toml` at the destination's head;
- the ids of the evidence on the trees the evaluation read (its own tree, and for sign-offs the trees of the members above it), an index-only read of `evidence_by_tree`;
- the change's provenance: the count and newest `recorded_at` of its records;
- **the destination's head commit only when the evaluation depended on it**: the policy reads `landing` or `batch` (the static check's `reads` set), or the last result was blocked by the preview (`conflicted`, `empty`, `destination`).

So a destination that moves without touching `reef-policy.toml` re-evaluates nothing for a policy that does not read `landing`: each pass reads the policy blob id once per destination, compares keys, and stops. Every proposal waiting for evidence or a sign-off keeps its key.

**Events the key cannot see** mark proposals instead (in memory): a lifecycle transition in the repository marks the proposals of that change's stack; a key or identity revocation, and a GC run that pruned evidence or artifacts, mark every proposal of the repository; an executor's claim after `no_executor` marks those `no_executor` proposals. A marked proposal is evaluated at the next pass whatever its key.

### When the service runs

One task per repository, the push worker's shape (`landing/push.rs`: `claim` with a dirty bit, `RunningGuard`, release when clean), started only while the repository has open proposals:

1. **Wakes**, all coarse (per repository): a lifecycle transition (`note_transitions` gains a second consumer), `AddEvidence`, `Provenance::record`, `Executor::changed`, an `OpEvents` bump for the repository, revocation and the GC hook. A wake sets the dirty bit; nothing polls.
2. **A pass**, at most one every 2 seconds per repository and 500 ms after the first wake: read the open proposals (`PROPOSED` lifecycle rows, at most 1,000, oldest proposal first), group them into stacks (a proposal is a stack head when no other open proposal for the same destination descends from it; read once per pass on the repository thread), compute each stack's key, and evaluate the stacks whose key changed or that are marked, **at most 16 per pass**; the rest wait for the next pass.
3. **An evaluation** is the gate's dry run over the stack head (`decide`, below) under a daemon-wide **background permit** (one automatic evaluation at a time across all repositories) and the usual policy turn, so automatic work uses at most one core and leaves three turns to requests.
4. **After it**: request the missing pairs (below), queue it if allowed and automatic (below), and store the status and key.

`decide` is `gate` up to its writes, returning the `Vec<Decided>` with each member's verdict and evaluation; `gate` calls it, then writes. It previews a stack along one chain (`preview_chain`) instead of `preview_above` per member, so a stack of *k* costs *k* rebases, which also speeds `reef land` on a stack.

### Evidence requests (decision 1)

Under `[landing] checks = "batch"` (the default: the setting that already says trusted evidence comes through executor jobs), every open proposal's missing `[[require]]` pairs on its own tree are requested, whether or not the destination lands automatically. Under `checks = "proposal"` nothing is requested: the repository's evidence comes from elsewhere (a CI system with its own key).

- **What**: for each member of the evaluated stack, the `missing` needs of its `[[require]]` results (never `failed` ones: a newest `fail` is a verdict, and is `needs`), grouped as the batch groups them, one job per kind and a separate one for a kind's whole check (an executor claims only a job whose kinds are all its own). The batch's `request` is extracted into one function both call.
- **How**: `Executor::submit` by revision (the member's queued commit and change), agent `reef-landing`, token `None`, `duplicate` false, `PROPOSAL_PRIORITY` 500 (below `BATCH_PRIORITY` 1,000, so landing batches run first; R3.21 replaces both), the executor's default wall time, and `[executor] queue_timeout` (1 hour) as the queue timeout. Dedupe is the executor's: a proposal and a batch on the same tree, two proposals on one tree, and a person's `reef job submit` on it share one open job.
- **Bounds**: at most 16 jobs per tree (the kinds a policy can name), and **at most 32 open jobs per repository** submitted by the service; past that, the oldest proposals' requests go first and the rest stay `pending` until jobs end. `TOO_MANY_JOBS` (the daemon-wide cap) is the same wait.
- **No executor**: before submitting, the service reads whether a job of the repository expired with `queue_timeout` after the last claim of any job of the repository (`job_claimed_since`'s table). If so it submits nothing for the repository, and each proposal that needs a job is `no_executor` with the hint "No executor answered for kinds test. Run one (`reef executor run`), or set `[landing] checks = \"proposal\"` if this repository's evidence does not come through executor jobs." The first claim of any job of the repository marks them, and requests resume. A laptop with no executor therefore holds at most one round of queued jobs per repository, which expire after an hour.
- **Infrastructure**: a job of the service that ended with no verdict for a pair (`failed`, `budget_exhausted`, expired by `deadline` or `attempts`, or done with only `error` or untrusted objects) is submitted once more. After a second, the pair is `needs` with the jobs named ("job_… failed twice on this tree: run it by hand with `reef job submit`"). Both facts come from the job table by tree, so nothing new is stored.
- **Cancellation**: when a proposal leaves review (withdrawn, declined, superseded, landed) or is amended, the service cancels its jobs that are still `queued` (never `running`: a running check's evidence is kept by tree and may serve a re-proposal) and whose tree no open proposal and no open batch trial still needs. Running jobs finish.
- **Attestors**: a request names kinds and targets, as R3.14's does. A need's attestor set decides whether the evidence counts, not who is asked; `docs/policy.md`'s sentence about "requests from the named attestor sets" is corrected.

### Automatic landing (decision 2)

`[landing] automatic = true` in `reef-policy.toml` at the destination's head (a boolean, default `false`, additive to format 1; an older daemon reports it `invalid_policy`). When an evaluation finds the stack head's prefix allowed, and `automatic` is true at the head it read:

1. **Which prefix.** `decide` gives each member's verdict bottom first, as the gate's prefix rule does. Let *m* be the first member that does not pass. If *m* is `waiting` (its only blocks are requested pairs with open jobs), **nothing is queued**: the stack waits for *m*'s evidence, since landing the members below *m* would rewrite *m* and its proposal would read `amended`. Otherwise (every member passes, or *m* is `needs`), the members below *m* are queued.
2. **The queue.** Through the gate's writes, as a request would (`enqueue`, one operation, the members' `PROPOSED → QUEUED` transitions with cause `landing`), with requester `reef-landing` and the landing row's new `automatic_key` set to the evaluated key. The gate runs again at the current head inside `enqueue`'s locks (its `recheck`), so a status that went stale between the evaluation and the write is caught there; a block then is a blocked row as for any request, deduplicated by S-125.
3. **The worker** is woken and lands as today: batches, tested trees, bisect, push.

**Re-queueing after a return.** A landing the service queued can come back (`dequeued`). Whether it is queued again is a table, not a branch per reason:

| Reason the landing returned | Queued again |
| --- | --- |
| `checks`, `policy`, `conflicted`, `empty`, `alone`, `unlanded_parents`, `amended`, `destination`, `failed` | Only after its key changes (`returned` until then). |
| `busy` | After 1 minute, at most 3 times per key. |
| `unavailable` | After an executor claims a job of the repository, or its key changes. |
| `cancelled` (withdrawn, landed by hand) | No: it is no longer a proposal. |

The rule reads the change's latest landing row and its `automatic_key`, so it holds across restarts.

### Stacks (decision 2)

The unit of evaluation is a **stack head**: an open proposal with no open proposal for the same destination above it. The head's stack is `land::stack` (R3.10, at most 32), judged with `decide`, so sign-offs count downward exactly as at the gate, and a policy-file member ends the prefix (`alone`). A member is evaluated as part of its head's stack only, never on its own; its status is its own verdict in that stack. A stack with a member that is not proposed for the destination is `needs` on its head ("change … below it is not proposed: `reef propose <head>`").

The service does not need #328 to work: it calls the gate's own judgment in the daemon. The ticket folds #328 in anyway (decision 6), since `EvaluatePolicy` over a stack is the same judgment as a read.

### `reef land` waits for requested evidence (decision 3)

`LandChange` on a proposal whose status is `waiting` waits, within `wait_ms`, until its status leaves `waiting` (a `Notify` per repository on status changes), then runs the gate as today. When the wait ends while it is still `waiting`, no blocked row is written: the response carries `waiting` (the status, with its pairs and jobs) and no `landing`, and `reef land` exits 6 (`LANDING_PENDING`), with `next` `reef land <id>`. `--no-wait` returns at once the same way. So one command covers both kinds of destination: with `automatic`, an agent proposes and may stop there, or run `reef land` to wait for the result; without it, `reef land` waits for the checks and lands.

### What the agent sees (decision 4)

- **`ProposalEntry.automatic`**: the status on `ListProposals` entries, which `reef proposal list` (one short column: `waiting test`, `queued`, `needs signoff`) and `reef proposal show` (the state, its message, pairs, jobs and `next`) print.
- **`reef propose`**: its closing line names what happens next from the destination's policy at its head: "lands automatically once policy allows it: `reef proposal show <id>`; `reef land <id>` waits for it" under `automatic`, and "`reef land <id>` once its checks pass (requested: test)" otherwise. MCP's `propose_change` returns the same sentence; `land_change` waits as `reef land` does. No new MCP tool.
- **`reef landing show`** prints "queued automatically" for a landing whose requester is `reef-landing`.
- **Operations**: the automatic queue is an operation like a request's (cause `landing`), so `wait_for_operations` with kinds `transition` and `land` on the change already follows it; job requests are not operations.
- **Not changed**: `reef here`, the agent guide and `reef describe` (#262 owns them now), and the root help.

### Telling the proposing agent (decision 5)

An agent that proposed onto an automatic destination may have stopped waiting. When automatic landing gives up on its change for a reason the agent can fix, the daemon writes **one R3.12 instruction** on the change, routed by R3.12's rules to the session that proposed it (`comments::target`: the proposal's session, else the latest provenance), and delivered by R3.12's pull: the harness hook at the turn's end and the next prompt, `reef comment next`, and `reef mcp`'s `wait_for_operations`.

- **When.** Only on a destination with `automatic = true`, and only for these outcomes, each a row of one table (`Instructed`), never a branch per reason:

  | Outcome | The instruction says |
  | --- | --- |
  | `needs` because a `[[require]]` pair **failed** on its own tree | which kinds and targets failed, the evidence ids, "fix the change and propose it again" |
  | `returned` with `checks` (the batch's culprit) | the batch, the kinds that failed on its tested tree, "fix it against the destination and propose again" |
  | `needs` or `returned` with `conflicted` | up to 5 conflicted paths, `reef rebase --onto <destination>`, then propose again |
  | `needs` because the change was amended after proposing | `reef propose` to put the new commit forward |
  | `returned` with `empty` | its edits are already on the destination: withdraw it |

  Not for what the agent cannot fix: a missing sign-off (a reviewer's), `no_executor` and infrastructure failures (the operator's), `busy` and `unavailable` (retried), `policy` blocks by a free rule (the message is the policy owners' and goes to `reef proposal show`), a `destination` problem.
- **The author.** Actor `daemon` (R3.09's `Actor::Daemon`, already in `TransitionActor`), agent `reef-landing`, no identity, no remote token: the record says the daemon wrote it. It is created `PENDING`, with R3.12's estimate stored and no confirmation step: the estimate gate protects a person paying for new work, and this is the agent's own proposal coming back to it. `reef comment show` prints "from reef-landing (daemon)".
- **The text is a fixed template** filled only with values Reef or the destination's owners chose: the change id, the destination, the reason, landing, batch, job and evidence ids, kinds and targets from the destination's policy, and conflicted paths quoted with escapes (Rust's `{:?}`), at most 5 and 200 bytes each. Never an evidence summary, an artifact, a job message, a rule's `message` or a description. Under 1 KiB, so R3.12's frame and limits hold (S-228).
- **Dedupe.** At most one per change, commit and outcome: the template's text is a function of them, and the service skips a write when the change already has a daemon instruction with the same `text_sha256` on the same commit. At most one unfinished daemon instruction per change: while one is `PENDING`, `DELIVERED` or `RUNNING`, a new outcome waits for the next key change. R3.12's limits (16 unfinished per change, 64 per session) still apply; a refusal is logged and skipped (S-229). R3.12's sweeps end it as for any instruction: `reproposed` marks it `DONE` when the agent proposes again; `change_final` cancels it if the change lands some other way.
- **Writes** go through `comments::create`'s path (its operation of kind `comment`, the comment lock, rows), from the automatic task after its evaluation, never under the lifecycle lock.

### `EvaluatePolicy` over a stack (decision 6, closes #328)

`EvaluatePolicyRequest.stack` (field 9, default false) judges the revision's stack as the gate would: the members `(::revision) ~ (::policy_revision)` (`trunk()` by default), at most 32, bottom first, each previewed along one chain onto the policy revision, sign-offs from above counted downward, a policy-file member ending the prefix (`alone`), the first block stopping the prefix and the members above reported `unlanded_parents`. Members need not be proposed: this is a read, and an agent asks before proposing. `policy` bytes work as for one change.

- **Response**: `EvaluatePolicyResponse.members` (`repeated StackMemberEvaluation`: `change_id`, `commit_id`, `position`, `passes`, `reason`, `message`, `evaluation`), bottom first; `evaluation` (field 1) stays the head's, so a client that ignores `members` reads what it read before. `prefix` is how many members would be queued now.
- **Same code**: the dry run, `judge_stack`, is what `decide` (the gate) and the automatic service call, given the destination commit and the policy source, so the read and the landing cannot disagree (#328's acceptance).
- **CLI**: `reef policy eval --stack [REV]` prints one line per member (`allow`, or the reason and what it lacks) and the prefix; the brief view keeps `prefix` and the first blocker. A one-change eval of a change with unlanded proposed ancestors says so in `hint`, with `next` `reef policy eval --stack <rev>`.
- `EvaluatePolicy` is in `NOT_TOOLS`; MCP is unchanged.

### The record: schema 23

```sql
-- 0023_automatic_landing.sql
ALTER TABLE landings ADD COLUMN automatic_key TEXT;  -- the evaluation key when the automatic service queued it; NULL for a request
```

Nothing else is stored. Statuses, keys and marks are in memory and rebuilt by one evaluation per open proposal after a start (bounded as any pass). Request attempts and the no-executor state are read from the job table by tree and repository. The re-queue rule reads `automatic_key` from the change's latest landing. `repo forget` and backups are unchanged (a column on an existing table).

  AUTOMATIC_STATE_UNSPECIFIED = 0;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_OFF = 1;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_PENDING = 2;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_WAITING = 3;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_READY = 4;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_QUEUED = 5;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_NEEDS = 6;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_NO_EXECUTOR = 7;
  AUTOMATIC_STATE_RETURNED = 8;
}

message AutomaticStatus {
  AutomaticState state = 1;
  string message = 2;                         // the gate's message, or the wait's
  repeated PolicyEvidenceNeed missing = 3;    // at most 20: requested pairs, or sign-offs needed
  int32 missing_total = 4;

  string reason = 7;                          // NEEDS and RETURNED: the gate's or landing's reason
  string next = 8;                            // a command line
  google.protobuf.Timestamp evaluated_at = 9;
}

### Threat model rows

- **S-221 Executor work by proposing.** Any caller that may propose (a remote `write` token included) makes the executor run the repository's checks. Mitigated: the same caller may already `SubmitJob`; only the policy's `[[require]]` pairs on the proposal's own tree, at most 16 jobs per tree and 32 open per repository, below batches, nothing while no executor answers, queued jobs cancelled when the proposal leaves.
- **S-222 Landing without a request.** A proposal onto an automatic destination lands with nobody asking. Accepted by design: the destination's owners turn it on in `reef-policy.toml` (a policy change needs their sign-off); the gate and batch are the same as for a request; the landing records `reef-landing` and the proposer.
- **S-223 A stale status.** A cached evaluation misses an input. Mitigated: the gate judges every queue at the current head, so staleness delays and never admits; events outside the key (revocation, pruning, transitions) mark proposals; a start rebuilds every status.
- **S-224 A queue and return loop.** A change queued, returned and queued again forever, re-running batches. Fixed: the re-queue table; `automatic_key` on the landing row survives restarts.
- **S-225 Background CPU.** Many proposals re-evaluated at once on a laptop. Mitigated: one background evaluation daemon-wide, 16 per pass, one pass per 2 seconds per repository, keys that skip destination moves.
- **S-226 Cancelling another requester's job.** Dedupe shares a job between the service, a batch and a person's `reef job submit`. Mitigated: only queued jobs the service submitted (agent `reef-landing`, the proposal's commit), only when no open proposal or batch trial needs the tree; a person who deduped onto one sees it `cancelled` with the reason and submits again (documented).
- **S-227 Starvation across repositories.** One repository's proposals fill the daemon-wide job cap. Mitigated: 32 open service jobs per repository, well under `max_open_jobs`.
- **S-228 A daemon instruction as an injection path.** An instruction reaches a model's context; text from evidence, jobs, rules or descriptions would let another party steer the agent. Fixed: a fixed template filled only with ids, the reason, kinds and targets from the destination's policy, and escaped conflicted paths; author `daemon` in the frame; no confirmation step only for this author.
- **S-229 Instruction spam.** Outcomes that flap would fill an agent's turns with instructions. Fixed: one per change, commit and outcome (by text hash), at most one unfinished per change, only on automatic destinations and only for outcomes the agent can fix; R3.12's per-change and per-session limits.

## Implementation notes

- **Order**: reef-policy; `decide` and the chain preview (the gate's tests run unchanged); `request` extracted (the batch's tests unchanged); the service with keys and wakes; automatic landing and re-queueing; `LandChange`'s wait; the API and CLI; the documents.
- **Lock order** stays the gate's: `decide` takes no lock; queueing goes through `enqueue` (create lock shared, then the lifecycle lock). The service never holds a lock across an evaluation or a submit; `submit` runs on the blocking pool.
- **Wakes are cheap**: each hook only sets a dirty bit and wakes the repository's task, and `note_transitions`' new consumer only enqueues (it runs under the lifecycle lock).
- **Tests use the paused clock** for timeouts and backoff, and `testing::no_automatic_snapshots` in new fixtures (#205).
- **Linux**: nothing is `cfg(target_os = "linux")`.
- **#262** (in parallel, API revision 37) puts the lifecycle step in `GetWorkspace`, and its `reef here` shows `land` for a proposed change. Under `[landing] automatic = true` that step is `wait`; whichever of the two PRs merges second adds that arm.

## Performance and laptop cost

- No new process. One task per repository while it has open proposals, idle (no timers, no polling) otherwise.
- A destination move with no policy change costs, per repository, one blob lookup per destination and one index-only read per open proposal; no evaluation. Target: under 5 ms for 100 open proposals.
- An evaluation costs what `reef land` costs (under 150 ms for a 50-file change; a stack of 32 with one chain preview). At most one runs at a time daemon-wide.
- Executor work: only the policy's missing pairs, deduped by tree, at most 32 open per repository; with no executor, one round of queued jobs per repository, expiring after an hour.
- Disk: one nullable column; jobs follow `[executor] job_retention`.
- A daemon start evaluates every open proposal once, 16 per pass per repository.

## Out of scope

- **Instructions for proposals onto destinations without `automatic`**, and for outcomes the agent cannot fix (sign-offs, executors): the status shows them.
- **The `stack` policy input** (R3.24), which can share `judge_stack`'s stack read.
- **A minimal check set** (R3.18), **budgets** (R3.20), **priority** beyond `PROPOSAL_PRIORITY` and `BATCH_PRIORITY` (R3.21).
- **Re-proposing an amended or rewritten change** (R3.11); **starting an executor** (Reef runs no agent and no executor).
- **Evidence for queued changes' tested trees**: R3.14's, unchanged.
- **`reef here`, the guide, `reef describe`**: #262.

## Risks

- **The gate split** touches R3.13's and R3.10's most branched function. Mitigated: `decide` returns exactly what `gate` judged, `gate` becomes `decide` plus the writes, and every landing and stack test runs unchanged.
- **A key that misses an input** delays a landing until a mark or another change (S-223). The tests cover each input; a pass also evaluates a proposal whose last evaluation is older than one hour, as a backstop, at the same per-pass bound.
- **Shared jobs** between a batch, the service and a person (S-226).
- **Numbers** collide with #262's at rebase; renumbered then.

## Choices this brief made, and why

- **The gate's own judgment, as a dry run**, because two evaluators would disagree; the cache can only delay, never admit.
- **Keys over cheap reads plus marks for the rest**, because evidence has no generation counter and a stored one would need every writer to bump it; the ids on a tree are an index-only read.
- **The head out of the key unless the policy reads it**, which is the ticket's "a moving destination does not re-evaluate every proposal".
- **Statuses in memory, one column on disk**, because everything else is derivable (the job table, the landing rows) and a start re-evaluates within the same bounds.
- **Requests tied to `checks = "batch"`**, the setting that already says evidence comes from executor jobs, so no new key.
- **Priority below batches**, so landings finish before new checks start.

## Departures in the implementation

Found while building it (2026-11-18):

1. **Numbers on this base.** `main` lacks #262's revision 37 and `0022`, so the code serves **API revision 37** and the database's version (its count of migrations) is **22**, with the migration named `0023_automatic_landing` as planned; the prose says 37 and 22. The second of the two PRs to merge renumbers.
2. **`reef propose`'s closing line is one sentence for both kinds of destination**: the client does not read the destination's policy, and the status is not judged yet when the proposal returns. It says that the daemon asks executors for the missing checks, that the change lands with no command where the policy says `automatic = true`, else `reef land` waits for the checks and lands it, and that `reef proposal show` says what it waits for. MCP is unchanged: `tools/list` is at its bound (25,286 bytes), so `propose_change` and `land_change` keep their descriptions, and `land_change` waits for the checks as `reef land` does.
3. **`LandChange`'s wait judges first when needed.** A proposal with no status yet, or `pending`, is marked and judged before the wait decides; `--no-wait` judges nothing and answers `waiting` only from a status already `waiting`. A change that is not `proposed` skips the wait, so its error is the gate's. In `reef land -o json` and MCP's `land_change`, a waiting answer is a landing with outcome `waiting` and the status's message, so the output schemas stay one shape.
4. **The gate still previews each member of a stack on its own** (`preview_above`, *k − 1* rebases for member *k*). The chain preview the brief planned would change R3.10's judgment code for a cost the service pays once per key change, at most 16 stacks a pass; it is left for a follow-up.
5. **Statuses of a stack.** A change stopped by a member below it that is `waiting` reads `waiting` too ("waits for <id> below it"); stopped by anything else it keeps the gate's `unlanded_parents`.
6. **The instruction outcomes** add `needs` for an empty change (its edits are already on the destination), with the `returned` one, and every template names the destination, cut to the same safe alphabet as the ids.
7. **`EvaluatePolicy` over a stack** names the destination with `policy_revision` as a bookmark (the trunk bookmark when empty), since the gate's judgment needs a local bookmark; its `evaluation` is the revision's own, and `reef policy eval --stack` prints the first refused change's evaluation when the stack stops below the revision.
8. **The no-executor state is tested as a rule** (`automatic::judge::tests`), not end to end: an unclaimed job expires after at least a minute of real time, which the integration tests do not pause.
9. **Review findings** (the PR's table) changed the design in six places:
   - **No executor is per kind.** One job of an unserved kind stays open per repository, so a newly started executor has something to claim and its claim lets the rest through, and a kind nobody serves never holds back another (finding 1).
   - **A problem at a stack's top never holds back its bottom.** When the gate refuses a change of the stack by name (amended, not proposed, proposed elsewhere), the changes below it are judged as their own stack (finding 3).
   - **Every change of a waiting stack asks for its own checks**, judged with the changes below it, so a stack's checks run side by side (finding 5). A change waiting on one below it, or on `no_executor`, holds the prefix back (finding 10).
   - **Errors and full slots are tried again after 30 seconds** (`retry_at`), and a failure to queue reads `needs`, `failed` (an unset identity has no retry) (finding 4). Marks a full pass leaves are kept for the next (finding 2).
   - **Wake-ups are per repository and keep a permit**: the executor and provenance record which repositories changed, revocations mark every proposal, and the task wakes at least hourly for the staleness backstop (findings 7, 8). GC marks proposals only when it pruned evidence or artifacts (finding 13).
   - **Queued changes keep a status** (`queued`), and the daemon's instructions are one per text per proposal (since its latest transition), which also covers an amended change, whose instruction names its visible commit (findings 6, 12).
