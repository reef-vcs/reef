# 0044. History index and query layer

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-30
- Ticket: R2.16

## Context

The [design](../design.md) says "history is a database. Agents ask structured questions such as which changes touched a given symbol in the last week and what their test outcomes were, and receive structured rows" (Query layer), and that jj's operation log stays authoritative while SQLite indexes it (Technology choices). [0003](0003-sqlite-for-daemon-metadata.md) put those indexes in the one metadata database. [0042](0042-symbol-index.md) built the symbol index as a per-repository cache instead, and [0043](0043-symbol-level-diff.md) matched definitions across a diff. Nothing remembered, per change, who wrote it, what became of it, and which files and symbols it touches. `ListOperations` walks the log and cannot filter by path or symbol, and `ListSymbols` answers for one tree.

## Decision

1. **The change is the unit, and its touches are its net diff.** A row per change id: its latest commit, its outcome, and what its latest commit changes against its parents' merged tree, as `reef show` would show it. A function added in one snapshot and removed in the next is not a touch, and a rebase changes nothing.
2. **Activity per (change, agent, workspace), by the direct-write rule.** First and last write, the operation count and the last operation. An operation writes a change directly when it created it, moved a working copy to its new commit (a snapshot, a move), or named its previous commit in Reef's describe, edit or abandon description. Every effect of another jj client's operation counts, since Reef cannot tell its intent. Other rewrites are rebases: they move the change's commit and add no activity. `agent` is `Operation.agent`, matched exactly, as `OperationFilter.agents` is. A rebase of a change the index never saw written adds no row.
3. **Four outcomes, read from the repository at head.** `OPEN` and `LANDED` (a visible commit in `::trunk()`), `ABANDONED` and `UNDONE` (hidden, by an undo for the latter). They are recomputed for every change a batch touches, from the repository reloaded at head, never accumulated, so backfill and catch-up in any order give the same answer. A move of `trunk()` lands or reopens up to 10,000 changes at once. `landed_at` is when the follower saw the landing; a change already landed when first seen has none. `divergent` is a flag, not an outcome. A squash merge on a forge, fetched, lands a new change: the agent's own stays open until abandoned.
4. **A per-repository cache file, `repos/<id>/history.sqlite3`, with no `ATTACH`.** Built like the symbol index's store: one writer and a few readers, WAL, `0600`, `meta.format`, no migrations, recreated (then backfilled) when unreadable, outside backup, deleted with the repository. This amends 0003 as 0042 did: rows are derived from the log, queries join large tables and must not take the metadata mutex lease heartbeats take, and a cache should not grow every backup. Touches keep their own interned symbol, name and path strings rather than joining `symbols.sqlite3`, because the symbol store evicts by size and is recreated when its extractors change, and history must outlive both.
5. **Following the log as subscriptions read it.** One task reads each open repository's operations after a stored frontier through `subscribe::feed`'s read, woken by `OpEvents` (and following each repository, so another jj writer's operations are polled). Effects are the API's, and an operation whose effects the API capped at 1,000 change entries is read again with a cap of 10,000, so a change it wrote is not lost behind its rebased descendants. How a change was hidden (by an undo or not) is recorded by change id apart from its row, and a visible change's commit is set from the repository at head, so the order operations are read in does not matter. A new or recreated index starts at the op heads and backfills newest first, in chunks, resumable, bounded by `[history] backfill_operations` (100,000) and `[history] retention` (90 days). Imported commits are dated by their committer time and left out past the retention. A follower more than 10,000 operations behind, or whose frontier a GC it did not see rewrote, restarts at the heads and catches up: a second resumable walk, run before any backfill and not bounded by `backfill_operations`, that stops at the old frontier, or by its end time when GC took its operations.
6. **GC's rewrite of operation ids is mapped.** `GcOutcome.rewritten` carries the old-to-new id map `reparent_mapped` builds. GC pauses the repository's follower from before its jj step until it has mapped the frontier, an unfinished backfill and every `last_operation_id` through it. Ids of abandoned operations become `NULL`. A sixth GC step deletes changes not open whose activity, landing and end are all past the retention, then the oldest down to `[history] max_size` (512 MiB), never one with activity in the last day.
7. **Touches computed by a throttled worker, with R2.15's matching.** One change at a time per daemon on the read pool, queued by the table itself (changes whose touches are not for their latest commit), newest first, each once it has been quiet 5 s or its touches are 60 s behind. Paths come from the diff against the parents (10,000 stored); symbols from `symbols::diff::compute` with every differing file as the page, no call sites or expansion, up to R2.15's 1,000 files (10,000 stored). Coverage per change: `COVERED`, `PARTIAL`, `TOO_LARGE` or `DISABLED`.
8. **A typed RPC, not SQL.** `HistoryService.QueryChanges` in `history.proto` (API revision 9). The file's schema is a cache's and changes with its format; caller SQL would be a contract nothing versions, and over R2.08's remote listener an injection and denial-of-service surface. Typed filters get page tokens, bounds and generated SDKs, and R4's store can serve the same RPC.
9. **Filters, grouping and coverage.** `symbols` (R2.15's `matches_expand`: a symbol, a suffix from a descriptor boundary, or a name; narrowed by display name in SQL, decided in Rust), `paths` (by whole components), `symbol_changes`, `outcomes`, `changes`, `include_empty`, and `agents`, `workspaces`, `since` and `until`, which apply to one activity row together. Fields AND, entries OR. Newest last write first, paged by `(last_at, id)`. `group_by` agent, outcome or workspace counts distinct changes per group, up to 1,000. Every answer carries `coverage`: the oldest write held, whether a backfill runs, operations not yet read, and changes pending; every row carries its touch state and symbol coverage. Two reasons: `HISTORY_DISABLED` and `INVALID_HISTORY_QUERY`.
10. **`ListSymbols` by name and symbol reads in path order, its filters and cursor in SQL.** The tree check stays outside SQL, since a tree's file list in SQL is the copy 0042 rejected. It runs in path order, through a `reef_path` collation registered on every connection, and stops one row past the page. `list_name` on `large` went from 387 ms to about 180 ms. Answers and page tokens are unchanged.
11. **`reef history` and an MCP tool, `query_changes`.** The tool takes paths, symbols, agents, outcomes, `since` and paging. "Who touched this" is then one call, not a page of `list_operations` plus a diff per change. `tools/list` rises to 21,191 bytes against a budget of 21,250.

## Consequences

- An agent asks which changes touched a path or symbol, who wrote them and whether they landed, in one call, and the answer says how complete it is.
- "Who wrote it" is whoever the operation names; `reef-agent` is not authentication. R3.04's provenance makes it durable and signed.
- History older than the operation log (trimmed by GC to the undo window) is lost with the file. It is not backed up.
- Effects past 10,000 change entries in one operation are not read: imports past it get no rows, and rebased descendants past it keep their old commit until they are next read.
- A catch-up that stops by time skips operations that ended in the same millisecond as the old frontier, after it; outcomes, read at head, stay right.
- Every session's `tools/list` costs 1,143 bytes more.

## Alternatives considered

- **Tables in `reef.sqlite3`.** One file, as 0003 says, but queries would share the metadata mutex and the index would grow every backup.
- **Attaching `symbols.sqlite3`.** Touches would vanish with the symbol store's evictions and recreations.
- **A row per operation.** About 1.4 million a day at the phase's load; activity rows grow with changes.
- **Touches per snapshot, or interdiffs.** They answer "what did the agent try", not "what touched this"; the operation log has that.
- **Raw read-only SQL.** See decision 8.
- **Extending `ListOperations` or `ListChanges`.** Neither is an index read, and both carry a revision or a walk the history has no use for.
- **Windows by operation range.** GC rewrites operation ids; times survive it.
- **No MCP tool.** The question R2.17 measures would stay a loop of calls.
